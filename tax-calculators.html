The Complete Guide to Tax Calculators

Taxes touch every paycheck, every freelance invoice, and every investment sale — yet most people only think about them in April. This hub collects 19 free tax calculators for W-2 employees, freelancers and gig workers, investors, landlords, and small business owners. Estimate what you owe, tune your withholding, plan quarterly payments, and compare tax strategies — all instantly, and all without your numbers ever leaving your browser.

For employees: keep more of every paycheck

The Paycheck Net Take-Home (2026) calculator breaks down exactly where each dollar of your salary goes: federal tax, state tax, Social Security, Medicare, and pre-tax deductions. If last April brought an unpleasant surprise — or a giant refund that was really an interest-free loan to the IRS — the W-4 Withholding Calculator helps you dial your allowances so your withholding lands close to your actual liability. The Tax Refund Estimator previews filing season before you file, the Bonus Tax Calculator shows how much of a bonus survives supplemental-wage withholding, and the RSU Tax Calculator estimates what vesting stock will cost you in taxes.

For the self-employed: stay ahead of quarterly deadlines

Freelancers do not get withholding — the IRS still wants its money four times a year. The Freelance 1099 Tax Estimator projects your total bill, the Self-Employment Tax Calculator isolates the Social Security and Medicare portion (both halves, since you are employer and employee), and the Quarterly Tax Calculator sizes each estimated payment so you avoid underpayment penalties. Choosing a business structure? The LLC Tax Calculator estimates pass-through liability while the S-Corp Tax Savings Calculator models the classic salary-vs-distribution split that can reduce self-employment tax once profits are high enough.

For investors, families, and movers

The Capital Gains Tax (2026) calculator separates short-term from long-term treatment before you sell, and the Roth Conversion Tax tool weighs the upfront tax cost of a conversion against decades of tax-free growth. Families can check the Child Tax Credit & EITC estimator, high-net-worth households can model the Estate & Lifetime Gift Tax, homeowners get the Property Tax & Mill Rate calculator, shoppers get US State Sales Tax, and anyone considering a move can compare all 50 states with the State Tax Relocation calculator.

How to pick the right tool

Employees: start with Paycheck Net Take-Home, then W-4 Withholding, then the Tax Refund Estimator. Freelancers and gig workers: 1099 Estimator, Self-Employment Tax, and Quarterly Tax — in that order. Investors: Capital Gains and Roth Conversion. Movers: State Tax Relocation before you sign a lease. And one honest note: these are estimates for planning, not professional tax advice. Tax law is intricate and changes yearly — verify anything consequential with a CPA or the IRS before you file or make irrevocable moves.

Tax Calculator FAQs

Straight answers to the questions people ask most about tax calculators.

Will I owe a penalty if I skip quarterly estimated tax payments?

Possibly. If you expect to owe $1,000 or more in tax after withholding and credits, the IRS generally requires quarterly estimated payments, and underpaying can trigger penalties plus interest. The Quarterly Tax Calculator sizes each payment; the usual safe harbors are paying at least 90% of the current year’s tax or 100% of last year’s tax (110% at higher incomes).

What is the difference between a tax credit and a tax deduction?

A deduction reduces the income you are taxed on; a credit reduces the tax itself, dollar for dollar. That makes credits far more powerful: a $2,000 credit cuts your bill by $2,000, while a $2,000 deduction only saves you $2,000 times your marginal rate. The Child Tax Credit & EITC calculator shows credits in action.

Should I adjust my W-4 withholding?

If you owed a large balance or received a huge refund last April, yes — your withholding is miscalibrated. A big refund means you gave the IRS an interest-free loan; a big bill means possible underpayment penalties. The W-4 Withholding Calculator helps you tune it so each paycheck lands closer to your true liability.

How are work bonuses taxed?

Bonuses are supplemental wages: employers typically withhold a flat 22% for federal tax on bonuses under $1 million. That is just withholding, not your final tax — the bonus ultimately gets taxed at your marginal rate when you file, so you may owe more or get some back. The Bonus Tax Calculator previews the take-home.

How much should a freelancer set aside for taxes?

A common rule of thumb is 25–30% of net profit in a separate account, but the right number depends on your bracket, state, and deductions. Run the Freelance 1099 Tax Estimator for your situation, then use the Quarterly Tax Calculator to schedule payments so April never ambushes you.

Can these tax calculators replace a CPA?

No. They are excellent for estimates, scenario planning, and catching withholding problems early, but they are not professional tax advice. For audits, business structure changes, equity compensation, or anything involving large sums, work with a licensed CPA or tax attorney.
