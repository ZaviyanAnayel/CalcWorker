How Exact Age Is Calculated

Born March 15, 1990. Today October 14, 2026:

  1. Years: 2026 - 1990 = 36, but birthday hasn't passed... wait, March already passed, so 36 years.
  2. Months: March to October = 6 months (plus day adjustment).
  3. Days: 15th to 14th = -1, borrow a month: 6 months, 29 days.

Result: 36 years, 6 months, 29 days. Total days: ~13,362 (accounting for 9 leap days).

The same birthday yields different totals at different precision: 36 years, 6 months, 29 days = 13,362 days = 1,908 weeks and 6 days = 320,688 hours. Financial and legal systems round differently: life insurers use "age nearest birthday" — born March 15, 1990, your insurance age flips to 37 on September 15, 2026, six months early — while most contracts use "age last birthday." A half-year age step can raise term-life premiums 4-8%, so the rounding rule carries a literal price tag. Always ask which convention a form uses before filling in your age.

Off-by-one errors cluster around month boundaries. March 15 to October 14 is 6 months and 29 days only because October has 31 days — borrow from a 30-day month and the day count shifts. Spreadsheet DATEDIF with the "md" unit gets this wrong often enough that auditors distrust it; the reliable method is total-days subtraction, then derive years and months from the day count. For legal filings (visa petitions, retirement applications), always state the as-of date — "36 as of October 14, 2026" — because the correct answer changes daily.

Leap Years and Edge Cases

  • Leap babies (Feb 29): Legally age on Feb 28 or Mar 1 in non-leap years (varies by jurisdiction).
  • Leap day count: Years divisible by 4, except centuries unless divisible by 400. 2000 was leap; 1900 wasn't.
  • Day-of-week: Jan 1, 1990 was a Monday - algorithms like Doomsday compute any date's weekday.
  • Time zones: "Age" for legal purposes uses local date at place of birth/event.

The Gregorian calendar's 400-year cycle contains exactly 97 leap days — not 100, because the century rule drops 1700, 1800, and 1900 — making the average year 365.2425 days. Feb 29 babies are 1-in-1,461 rarities: someone born February 29, 2000 turned "21" having seen only five or six actual birthdays. Jurisdictions split on the legal birthday: England and Wales say March 1, much of the US says February 28, Taiwan's civil code says February 28. The difference decides exactly when you can vote, drink, or sign contracts — it is not trivia.

Weekday math has its own anchor system. 2026's "doomsday" — the weekday shared by 4/4, 6/6, 8/8, 10/10, and 12/12 — is Saturday, so October 10, 2026 is a Saturday and October 14 is a Wednesday. The Doomsday algorithm computes any date's weekday in seconds once you know the year's anchor. Time-zone edge cases matter legally too: a baby born 11:30 PM on December 31 in New York is a January 1 baby in UTC — tax dependent eligibility and age cutoffs follow local date at the place of birth, so record the time zone, not just the date.

Days Between Dates

Days = End Date - Start Date (exclusive of start, inclusive of end)

Project: Jan 10 to Apr 10, 2026: Jan (21) + Feb (28) + Mar (31) + Apr (10) = 90 days.

Common uses: Pregnancy weeks, project timelines, warranty periods, visa stays, invoice aging, statute of limitations.

The Schengen 90/180 rule is the highest-stakes day count in travel: at most 90 days inside any rolling 180-day window. As of October 14, 2026, look back to April 17, 2026 and count every day physically present — hit 91 and you're overstaying, with fines and multi-year entry bans as the penalty. The EES electronic system now tracks this automatically, so "I lost count" stopped working as an excuse. Dedicated short-stay calculators exist precisely because manual counting across a rolling window is brutally error-prone.

Finance runs on the same arithmetic with money attached. Invoice aging buckets (current, 30, 60, 90+ days past due) drive collections priority — a $50K invoice sliding from 60 to 90 days past due typically moves from "reminder" to "collections call" and gets reserved against bad debt. Warranty math is exclusive/inclusive sensitive: "1 year from purchase" on October 14, 2026 ends October 13, 2027 (365 days later, since 2027 isn't a leap year) — file the claim on the 14th and you're exactly one day late.

Practical Age Applications

  • Retirement planning: Exact age determines Social Security timing, RMDs, Medicare eligibility.
  • Insurance: Premiums step at half-year and yearly age marks.
  • School cutoffs: Districts use specific dates (e.g., "5 by Sept 1").
  • Milestone countdowns: Days until 18, 21, 30, 65 - legal and personal landmarks.
  • Pet/human age: Dog years ~ 15 (year 1) + 9 (year 2) + 5 each after - better than 7x.

Social Security's full retirement age is birth-year specific: born 1960 or later, it's 67. Claiming at 62 cuts benefits 30% for life, while each year delayed past 67 adds 8% up to age 70 — a 24% boost. On a $2,000/month full benefit, that's $1,400 at 62 versus $2,480 at 70: a $1,080/month gap, $12,960/year, for life. The exact-age calculation matters because benefits are computed by month — file even one month early and the reduction is permanent, which is why the SSA lets you specify your exact filing month.

SECURE 2.0 moved the required minimum distribution age to 73 (75 if born 1960 or later) — miss an RMD and the penalty is 25% of the amount not withdrawn. Medicare eligibility hits at 65 with a 7-month initial enrollment window (3 months before through 3 months after your 65th-birthday month); enroll late and Part B premiums rise 10% per year delayed, permanently. School cutoffs are equally exact: "5 by September 1" means an August 31 birthday starts kindergarten a full year before a September 2 birthday — an 11% age gap inside the same classroom.
