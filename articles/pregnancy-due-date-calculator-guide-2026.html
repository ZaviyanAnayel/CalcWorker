Naegele's Rule: The Due Date Formula

Due Date = First Day of Last Period + 7 Days - 3 Months + 1 Year

Example: LMP January 10, 2026: +7 days = Jan 17, -3 months = Oct 17, +1 year = October 17, 2026.

Only ~5% of babies arrive on the due date; most come within 2 weeks either side. First-trimester ultrasound (crown-rump length) dates more accurately than LMP when cycles are irregular.

Irregular cycles need a correction: with a 35-day cycle, ovulation lands around day 21 — a week later than the 28-day assumption — so add 7 days to Naegele's result. LMP January 10 with a 35-day cycle → October 24, not October 17. IVF dating is exact, no rule needed: add 266 days to a day-5 blastocyst transfer (268 for day-3). A March 1 day-5 transfer gives a November 22 due date, accurate to the day.

Accuracy in numbers: ~5% arrive on the date; ~80% arrive between 38 and 42 weeks. First-trimester ultrasound (crown-rump length) is accurate to ±5-7 days and overrides LMP whenever they disagree by more than 7 days — which happens in roughly 1 in 4 pregnancies. Third-trimester scans run ±3 weeks, too fuzzy to redate; never let a late scan move your due date.

Trimester Breakdown

TrimesterWeeksKey Developments
First1-13Neural tube, heart forms; fatigue, nausea peak
Second14-27Movement felt ~18-20 wks; anatomy scan ~20 wks
Third28-40Rapid growth; lungs mature; birth prep

Full term = 39-40 weeks. "Early term" (37-38) is fine but 39+ is ideal for lung and brain development when delivery timing is elective.

The trimester divisions are medical, not just organizational: miscarriage risk runs ~10-15% in weeks 1-12, then drops to ~1-3% after week 13 — the milestone behind the "wait until 12 weeks to announce" custom. Viability begins around 24 weeks with 50-70% NICU survival, improving ~3-5% per additional day. The 20-week anatomy scan detects roughly 70-80% of major structural anomalies, which is why it anchors the second trimester.

Trackable markers along the way: hCG doubles every 48-72 hours in early pregnancy — two blood draws 2 days apart confirm a viable trend. Nausea typically peaks at week 9 and fades by weeks 12-14 as the placenta takes over hormone production. From week 20, fundal height in centimeters roughly equals weeks (24 cm ≈ 24 weeks, ±2) — a tape measure your provider uses at every visit.

Key Milestones and Appointments

  • Week 8-10: First prenatal visit, dating ultrasound
  • Week 11-13: NT screening / NIPT bloodwork
  • Week 20: Anatomy scan - checks organs, growth, placenta
  • Week 24-28: Glucose tolerance test (gestational diabetes screen)
  • Week 28+: Biweekly visits; 36+: weekly visits
  • Week 41: Discussion of induction if overdue

What each test actually screens, with numbers: NIPT from week 10 detects trisomy 21 at ~99% sensitivity from a blood draw; nuchal translucency at 11-13 weeks flags risk if the fold exceeds 3.5 mm; the glucose challenge at 24-28 weeks uses a 140 mg/dL 1-hour cutoff — above it triggers the 3-hour diagnostic test. Group B strep swab at 35-37 weeks comes back positive in ~25% of women, which simply means antibiotics during labor, not a problem.

Kick counts start at week 28: time how long 10 movements take — expect under 2 hours; consistently longer warrants a call, not panic. Plan on ~12-14 prenatal visits for a low-risk pregnancy; high-risk adds growth scans every 3-4 weeks from week 28. The Tdap vaccine at 27-36 weeks passes pertussis antibodies to the baby — one shot protecting two people.

Month-by-Month Prep Checklist

  1. Months 1-3: Start prenatal vitamins (folic acid), choose a provider, avoid alcohol/smoking, share news when ready.
  2. Months 4-6: Plan the nursery budget, research childcare costs, start a baby emergency fund ($2,000-$5,000).
  3. Months 7-8: Take birthing/parenting classes, install the car seat, pack the hospital bag, finalize parental leave paperwork.
  4. Month 9: Rest, keep appointments weekly, have the birth plan and support contacts ready.

Money math, early: an average US vaginal birth is billed $13,000-18,000 (C-section $22,000-30,000); insured out-of-pocket typically lands $2,000-5,000 — call your insurer by month 4 to confirm your number, not the national average. Childcare averages ~$1,200/month nationally ($1,800+ in coastal cities); start waitlists by month 5, because quality centers run 6-12 month queues and "we'll figure it out later" is how parents end up with a $2,000/month backup plan.

Safety stats worth acting on: ~70% of car seats are installed incorrectly — book a free inspection at a fire station or hospital by week 36. Interview 2-3 pediatricians by week 34; ask specifically about after-hours coverage and hospital affiliation. Write the birth plan as a one-page preference sheet (pain management, cord cutting, skin-to-skin) — nurses read one page; nobody reads five.
