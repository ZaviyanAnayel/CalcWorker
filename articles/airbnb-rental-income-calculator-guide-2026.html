Revenue Formula

Annual Revenue = Nightly Rate × 365 × Occupancy Rate

$185/night × 365 × 65% occupancy = $43,891/year. Research your market's actual occupancy — national averages (~50-60%) mislead; top listings hit 75%+, weak ones 35%.

Seasonality: Model monthly, not annually. A ski condo at 90% winter / 30% summer needs cash reserves for the off-season.

Test the ADR-versus-occupancy tradeoff with the original numbers: $220/night × 55% occupancy = $44,165/year versus $185 × 65% = $43,891/year — nearly identical revenue. But the $185 listing hosts ~237 stays versus ~201, meaning 18% more cleanings, more wear, more guest messages. Higher ADR with lower occupancy earns the same money with less turnover cost and less hassle. When in doubt, price up, not down — discounts buy occupancy you don't want.

Compare listings with RevPAR (revenue per available room = ADR × occupancy). At $185 × 65%, RevPAR = $120.25. A $300 ADR at 30% occupancy ($90 RevPAR) loses to $150 at 70% ($105 RevPAR) — the "luxury" listing earns less per night of availability. Professionals buy and price on RevPAR, because it fuses price and occupancy into the single number that actually predicts revenue.

Cleaning turnover is the silent killer — 80 turnovers at $90 each. Self-cleaning saves it but costs your weekends.

Co-host math kills most STR dreams on paper: 22% of $43,891 = $9,656/year in management fees. Subtract that from the $8,374 net profit and the co-hosted property loses $1,282/year. Third-party management only pencils above roughly $70,000 in gross revenue, where the fee leaves room for profit. Below that line, the honest choices are self-manage or don't play — paying 22% to lose money is the most common STR mistake in 2026.

Direct bookings shift the economics: your own site ($500/year hosting + $30/month channel manager = $860/year) versus the 3% platform fee ($1,317 on $43,891). At this revenue level it's close — but every repeat guest who books direct costs 0% at the margin, and you own the customer relationship instead of renting it from a platform. Shifting just 10% of bookings direct saves ~$130/year and builds an asset the platform can never take away.

Regulation Risk

  • City bans/caps: Many cities now cap STR nights (e.g., 90-180/year) or require permits and primary-residency.
  • HOA bans: Check CC&Rs — HOAs increasingly prohibit rentals under 30 days.
  • Tax: 14-day rule: rent ≤14 days/year = tax-free income. Beyond that, Schedule E with depreciation.
  • Insurance: Standard homeowner's policies exclude commercial STR activity — get a proper rider.

Underwrite the downside: Can the property cash-flow as a long-term rental if STR rules change? If not, you are speculating on regulation.

Verify the current ordinance — not last year's blog post. Cities now cap STR nights at 90-180/year, require owner-occupancy, or demand permits costing $500-$2,000 annually. A 90-night cap at $185/night = $16,650 maximum gross — the entire $43,891 model dies on one line of municipal code. Check the city clerk's site, call the planning office, and read the actual ordinance text before making an offer. Sellers' pro-formas never include the regulation that bans the strategy.

Price in grandfather risk: permits can be revoked, capped, or non-renewed — "legal today" is not "legal in 2028." Underwrite the long-term-rental fallback first: if 12-month rents cover PITI plus expenses, the STR is pure upside and regulation is a haircut, not a catastrophe. If the property needs STR income to survive, you're not investing in real estate — you're speculating on municipal politics.

Boosting Profitability

  1. Dynamic pricing tools (PriceLabs, Beyond) typically lift revenue 10-20%.
  2. Direct bookings via your own site avoid the 3% fee and build repeat guests.
  3. Mid-term rentals (30+ days, traveling nurses) cut turnover costs dramatically.
  4. Amenities that pay: hot tubs, fast wifi, EV chargers, and pet-friendly policies command premiums.

Two policy tweaks worth $6,600/year with zero renovation. Enforce a 2-night minimum: 80 one-night stays ($7,200 in cleaning) become ~40 stays ($3,600) — $3,600 saved overnight. Add a $75 pet fee × 40 pet-friendly stays = $3,000 in pure margin. Combined: $6,600/year from settings changes. Turnover count is the variable you control most directly; every stay you eliminate is $90 kept.

Consider the mid-term pivot: traveling-nurse contracts at $3,500/month × 3 months = $10,500 with one turnover and near-zero vacancy between contracts. Thirty-plus-day stays on Airbnb or Furnished Finder cut cleaning costs ~90%, command stable monthly rates, and dodge most STR night-cap laws (30+ days counts as long-term housing in most cities). It's less glamorous than nightly STR — and often more profitable per hour of your time.
