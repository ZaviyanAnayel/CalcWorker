Pace, Speed, and Splits

Pace (min/mile) = 60 / Speed (mph) | Time = Distance / Speed

Conversions: 8:00/mile = 7.5 mph. 5:00/km = 8:03/mile. A marathon in 4:00:00 needs 9:09/mile (9:09 x 26.2 = 240 min).

Negative splits win: Running the second half slightly faster than the first produces better times and better experiences than going out hard - plan even or slightly negative splits.

Conversion shortcuts worth memorizing: 6:00/km = 9:39/mile (multiply km pace by 1.609); a 400m track lap at 8:00/mile pace takes 1:59; treadmill runners should set 1% grade to approximate outdoor effort — 0% at 8 mph runs ~7-10% easier than road. Belt calibration drifts too: treadmill speed can read 0.2-0.3 mph high, which is why "treadmill PRs" deserve an asterisk and a track verification.

GPS error budget: consumer watches measure distance within ±1-3%, so a "26.2" might be 25.9-26.5 — always race by the course markers, not the watch, in the final miles. Tree cover, tall buildings, and tunnels spike the error; the fix is a measured mile on a track (4 laps in lane 1 = exactly 1 mile... well, 1600m — close enough) to calibrate your true easy pace.

Predicting Race Times: Riegel Formula

T2 = T1 x (D2 / D1)^1.06

Example: 5K in 25:00. Predicted 10K = 25 x (10/5)^1.06 = 25 x 2.083 = 52:05. Half marathon approx 1:56; marathon approx 4:02.

Riegel assumes adequate training for the distance - undertrained runners fade worse than predicted, especially at the marathon.

Run Riegel backward as a diagnostic: marathon 4:02 (242 min) → predicted 5K = 242 x (5/42.2)^1.06 = 242 x 0.1042 = 25.2 min. If your actual 5K is 28:00, your endurance is underdeveloped relative to speed — prioritize long runs. If it's 23:30, you're speed-limited and need intervals. The gap between predicted and actual is a training prescription written in arithmetic.

Exponent tuning for honesty: 1.06 fits well-trained runners; bump to 1.08-1.10 if undertrained for the distance (it predicts slower, more achievable times). Beyond the marathon, Riegel breaks down — ultrarunners use 1.12-1.15, and predicting a 50K from a 5K is fiction. Golden rule: predict only from your longest recent race, never from a PR two years old.

25:00 5K runner (8:03/mile): Easy 9:30-10:30/mile; tempo ~8:25/mile; intervals at 7:45-8:03/mile.

Cross-check pace zones with heart rate: easy = under 75% max, tempo = 85-90%, intervals = 92-97%. The talk test is the field version: full sentences = easy, short phrases = tempo, single words = threshold. If the pace chart says "easy" but you can't talk, the pace is wrong for today's heat, hills, or fatigue — run the effort, not the number. Charts describe fitness; your body describes today.

Weekly shape for 30 miles/week: ~24 easy, 4 tempo, 2 interval — the 80/20 split expressed in mileage. Add 4-6 x 20-second strides after one easy run weekly for neuromuscular sharpness without fatigue; they cost nothing and pay in turnover. Cap hard days at 2-3 per week — the other 4-5 days are genuinely easy or full rest, no "kinda hard" middle ground.

Sub-25 5K Plan Snapshot

  1. Weeks 1-4: Build to 15-20 miles/week, all easy.
  2. Weeks 5-8: Add one tempo run (20 min) + one interval session (6x400m) weekly.
  3. Weeks 9-10: Peak with a 5K-pace workout (3x1 mile), then taper.
  4. Race: First mile at 8:10, middle at 8:00, kick the last 0.1.

The interval session, fully specified: 6 x 400m at 1:55-2:00 (faster than 8:00 race pace) with 90-second jog recoveries, after a 10-minute warm-up. Progression: weeks 5-6 extend to 8 x 400m; weeks 7-8 switch to 3 x 1 mile at 8:00-8:05 with 3-minute jogs. If you can't hit the splits two sessions running, you're under-recovered — cut the session short, don't grind it; fitness is built by completing workouts, not surviving them.

Race-day execution: 10-minute easy warm-up plus 4 strides, first 400m at 8:10 (adrenaline lies to everyone), settle to 8:00 through mile 2, then race the final mile — most sub-25 breakthroughs come from a 7:40-7:50 last mile, not a suicidal first. No fueling needed under 30 minutes; take water only if it's hot, and pin the bib the night before.
