Merchant cash advances hide brutal pricing behind "factor rates." A $50K advance repaid at $65K over 6 months is a 1.30 factor - which annualizes to roughly 60-80% APR. A 25% APR online term loan costs a fraction of that for the same capital. Whenever a lender quotes a factor instead of a rate, annualize it before comparing; the factor format exists to make expensive money look cheap.

SBA Express is the overlooked middle lane: up to $500K, decisions in about 36 hours, rates around prime plus 4.5-6%. Faster than a standard 7(a), far cheaper than online lenders. Borrowers who assume SBA means "slow" and online means "fast" miss the product built exactly for their situation.

Payment Example: $150,000 Loan

Payment = P x [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1]

$150K at 9%, 7 years (84 months): r = 0.0075. Payment = $2,405/mo. Total paid: $202,020. Interest: $52,020.

SBA version at 7.5%, 10 years: Payment $1,781/mo, total interest $63,720 - lower payment, more total interest. Match term to the asset's life.

Early payments are interest-heavy, which softens the tax bill. On the $150K loan at 9%, the first $2,405 payment splits into $1,125 interest (150,000 x 0.0075) and only $1,280 principal. Year-one interest runs about $12,900 - deductible at a 21% tax rate, worth roughly $2,700 in tax shield. Amortization front-loads interest; the tax code front-loads the deduction to match.

Small overpayments attack principal when the interest portion is fattest. Adding $200/month to the $150K, 9%, 7-year loan pays it off roughly 8 months early and saves about $4,000 in interest. Early extra dollars kill the most future interest because they shrink the balance that every subsequent interest calculation is based on.

Qualification: The 5 C's

  1. Credit: 680+ for banks/SBA; 600+ for online lenders.
  2. Cash flow: DSCR (debt service coverage ratio) 1.25x+ - income must exceed payments by 25%.
  3. Collateral: Equipment, real estate, or receivables backing the loan.
  4. Character: Business history, industry experience.
  5. Conditions: Loan purpose and economic environment.

DSCR example: $200K annual cash flow, $150K debt payments: DSCR = 1.33x. Qualifies.

DSCR sets your borrowing ceiling directly. With $200K annual cash flow and a 1.25x minimum, maximum annual debt payments are $160K - $13,333/month. At 9% over 7 years each $1,000 borrowed costs about $16.09/month, so $13,333 supports roughly $829K in total borrowing. Work backward from cash flow to loan size before falling in love with a property or equipment purchase.

Pre-application cleanup pays real money. Pushing business card utilization under 30% and avoiding new inquiries for 90 days can lift a score from 640 to 680 - worth 2+ APR points. On $150K over 7 years, 7% versus 9% saves about $12,000 in total interest. Ninety days of discipline buys a cheaper loan for seven years.

True Cost: Beyond the Rate

  • Origination fees: 1-5% of loan amount.
  • SBA guarantee fee: Up to 3.5% on 7(a) loans.
  • Factor rates: Online lenders quoting "1.15 factor" = 15% for the term, which annualizes to 30-80% APR on short terms. Always convert to APR.
  • Prepayment penalties: SBA: none after 3 years; some online loans penalize early payoff.

The note rate is advertising; APR is the price. A $150K loan at 9% with a 3% origination fee ($4,500) plus $2,000 in closing costs carries an effective APR near 10.1% over 7 years. Compare loans on APR and total cost, never on the quoted rate alone - two "9% loans" with different fee loads are different loans.

Prepayment penalties need their own break-even. A 5% penalty on a $100K balance costs $5,000; refinancing from 9% to 7% saves about $2,000 per year. The refinance needs two and a half years just to recover the penalty - only refinance if you will hold the new loan well past that point, or negotiate the penalty away upfront.

Frequently Asked Questions

680+ for SBA/bank loans at good rates; 600-680 for online lenders at higher rates; under 600 is very difficult.

Debt Service Coverage Ratio = annual cash flow / annual debt payments. Lenders want 1.25x+ (25% cushion).

SBA 7(a): lower rates, longer terms, slower (60-90 days), more paperwork. Bank: faster for established borrowers with strong credit.

Online lenders quote total repayment as principal x factor (e.g., 1.20). Convert to APR for honest comparison - short terms make factor rates brutally expensive.

Traditional loans usually need 2+ years of revenue. Startups: SBA microloans, business credit cards, equipment financing, or revenue-based lenders.
