Calories Per Gram

Protein: 4 cal/g | Carbs: 4 cal/g | Fat: 9 cal/g | Alcohol: 7 cal/g

Fat's 9 cal/g is why small portions of oils, nuts, and butter add up fast - a tablespoon of olive oil (14g) is 126 calories you barely notice.

Alcohol math, because it hides: 12 oz of 5% beer ≈ 14g alcohol x 7 cal = 98 cal, plus ~10g carbs (40 cal) ≈ 140 cal; two IPAs ≈ 400 cal with zero satiety. Sugar alcohols (2.4 cal/g) and soluble fiber (~2 cal/g) explain why "net carb" labels undercount energy — a 20g-fiber bar still carries ~40 hidden calories. The 4/4/9/7 figures are averages; your gut extracts slightly more or less, but the label math is what counts.

Label law reality: the FDA allows ±20% error, so a "200-calorie" bar may legally be 240; anything under 5 cal per serving can round to zero (cooking spray, mints). Restaurant dishes are worse — independent lab tests of chain meals routinely find 200-300 cal overages on 800-cal listings, a ±25-50% swing. When eating out, mentally add 20% to the menu number; you'll be right more often than the menu is.

2,200-calorie fat-loss example (180 lb): Protein 180g (720 cal), Fat 65g (585 cal), Carbs = (2,200 - 1,305) / 4 = 224g.

Second worked example — 140-lb woman, 1,700-cal fat loss: protein 140g (560 cal), fat 50g (450 cal), carbs = (1,700 - 1,010) / 4 = 172g. Note the order of operations: protein set from body weight first, fat second, carbs absorb the remainder. The order matters because protein and fat have physiological floors (muscle retention, hormones) while carbs are the flexible fuel — cutting carbs never breaks anything that protein and fat don't already cover.

Alternative splits keep the same skeleton: keto caps carbs at 20-50g and fills the rest with fat (1,700 cal → 140g protein, 30g carbs, 117g fat); high-carb/low-fat inverts it. Both work when protein and calories match — the trials say so repeatedly. Pick the one you can sustain for 12 straight weeks, not the one trending this month; adherence explains ~80% of diet outcomes.

Protein: The Priority Macro

  • Set protein first - it preserves muscle, maximizes satiety, and has the highest thermic effect (~25% of its calories burned in digestion).
  • Distribute across 3-5 meals: 30-50g per meal beats one giant protein dinner.
  • Best sources: lean meats, Greek yogurt, eggs, legumes, whey.
  • Vegetarians: combine legumes + grains and consider 10-15% higher targets (lower digestibility).

The leucine trigger: ~2.5-3g leucine per meal maximally stimulates muscle protein synthesis — that's 30g whey, 40g chicken breast, or 6 eggs. Adults over 60 face anabolic resistance and need ~40g+ protein per meal to get the same trigger that 25g gives a 30-year-old — the "small senior portions" habit works directly against them. Two 20g-protein meals and a big dinner is the classic pattern that fails; redistribute first.

Thermic math: 2,200 cal at 30% protein (165g) vs 15% (82g) — the extra 83g at ~25% thermic effect ≈ 80 cal/day burned in digestion, ~2,400 cal/month, roughly 0.7 lb of fat-equivalent per quarter. Satiety trials add more: high-protein breakfasts cut subsequent intake 100-200 cal/day. The effect compounds monthly into real weight difference without any extra willpower.

Tracking Without Obsession

  1. Weigh food for 2-4 weeks to calibrate your eye - then you can estimate.
  2. Hit protein and calories daily; let carbs/fat flex.
  3. Pre-log restaurant meals using chain nutrition data.
  4. One untracked meal won't ruin progress - weekly averages rule.
  5. If tracking stresses you, switch to plate method: half veg, quarter protein, quarter carbs + thumb of fat.

Hand portions when the scale stays home: palm = 25-30g protein, cupped hand = 20-30g carbs, fist = 1 cup vegetables, thumb = 1 tbsp fat/oils. Accuracy runs ±20-30% versus weighing — fine for maintenance, too loose for a contest cut. Restaurant rule of thumb: protein = two palms, carbs = one fist, and assume 1-2 tbsp of hidden cooking oil (120-240 cal) on anything sauteed, roasted, or "glazed."

Exiting the tracking phase: reverse-diet by adding ~100 cal/week until weight stabilizes at the new maintenance — typically 4-8 weeks of deliberate eating up. Keep weighing 2-3x/week at goal; a 3-lb drift triggers one fully tracked week to recalibrate the eye. Most successful maintainers track loosely for 3-6 months post-diet, then run on calibrated habits — the scale taught them, then they graduate.
