Your Payout Options

Most pensions offer a lump sum (one payment you invest yourself) or a monthly annuity (payments for life). Common annuity flavors:

  • Single life: highest payment, stops at your death
  • 50% joint & survivor: lower payment, spouse gets 50% after you die
  • 100% joint & survivor: lowest payment, spouse continues full amount
  • Period certain: guaranteed minimum years of payments
Breakeven Years ≈ Lump Sum ÷ Annual Annuity Payment

Two flavors the original list skipped: the "pop-up" joint annuity and the COLA rider. A pop-up option pays the reduced joint amount (say $2,160/month) while both spouses live, then pops back up to the $2,400 single-life amount if your spouse dies first — typically costing only $30-$60/month versus the standard joint option. A 2% annual COLA rider might cut the starting payment from $2,400 to about $1,950, a $450/month haircut that compounds in your favor over decades.

Also ask about the level-income (Social Security integration) option: the plan pays roughly $3,100/month until 62, then drops to about $1,900 when Social Security is assumed to start — smoothing total income. The trap: the drop happens at 62 whether or not you actually claim. Delay Social Security to 70 and you face an 8-year income gap. Cash-balance plans work differently still: a hypothetical account (say $400,000) credited with interest each year, converted to an annuity at retirement using the plan's factor.

Breakeven Math: $400,000 Lump Sum vs $2,400/Month

Scenario: Age 65. Lump sum $400,000 or $2,400/month ($28,800/year) single life.

Simple breakeven: $400,000 ÷ $28,800 = 13.9 years (age 79). But that ignores investing the lump sum:

  • If you invest the lump sum at 6%: it funds $28,800/year withdrawals for about 22 years (to age 87)
  • At 4% returns: about 18 years (to age 83)
  • Annuity wins if you live past ~87 (at 6%) — and it is guaranteed

The annuity is longevity insurance; the lump sum is flexibility and legacy. A 65-year-old man has ~50% odds of reaching 85; a woman ~60%.

The annuity's secret weapon is mortality credits. A $400,000 lump sum spread over a 20-year life expectancy is $20,000/year of principal — yet the annuity pays $28,800. The extra $8,800/year comes from interest plus the funds left behind by participants who die early. That is why no safe-withdrawal strategy matches an annuity payout rate: the 4% rule on $400,000 yields $16,000/year while the annuity pays 7.2%. You are being paid partly out of other people's forfeited benefits, and that subsidy grows more valuable the longer you live.

Run the "what if" extremes before deciding. A retiree in poor health with a 10-year life expectancy collects $288,000 total from the annuity versus $400,000 cash — the lump sum wins by $112,000 plus legacy value. A centenarian collects $1,008,000 over 35 years. Also know that lump-sum offers are computed with IRS corporate-bond segment rates: when rates rose in 2022-2023, lump sums shrank roughly 15-25% versus their 2021 peaks. A 2026 offer can be meaningfully smaller than the identical benefit's 2021 lump sum even though the monthly annuity never changed.

The Survivor Decision

OptionMonthlyAnnualTrade-off
Single life$2,400$28,800Spouse gets $0
50% survivor$2,160$25,920Spouse gets $1,080/mo
100% survivor$1,950$23,400Spouse keeps $1,950/mo

The 100% option costs $450/month ($5,400/year) — effectively buying a $1,950/month life annuity for your spouse. Compare that to life insurance: a $300K 20-year term policy at 65 costs ~$400-$600/month. Sometimes single life + term insurance beats the survivor option.

The age gap between spouses changes the math more than most expect. If your spouse is 10 years younger, the 100% survivor option might pay around $1,830/month instead of $1,950 — an extra $120/month haircut because the expected payout period stretches years longer. Conversely, a spouse close in age gets a milder reduction. Ask your plan administrator for quotes at each survivor percentage (25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) rather than accepting the three standard options; the price curve is rarely linear, and the 75% option is often the sweet spot.

Consider the period-certain alternative: a 10-year certain single-life annuity might pay $2,310/month versus $2,400 — only $90/month less — while guaranteeing at least $277,200 to your heirs if you die in year 3. For the term-insurance arbitrage, price it precisely: single life $2,400 minus 100% joint $1,950 = $450/month ($5,400/year) of "survivor premium." A healthy 65-year-old might secure a $250,000 15-year term policy for $250-$350/month, pocketing $100-$200/month while keeping the full $2,400 if the spouse dies first. Get both quotes in writing before the irrevocable election.

Lump Sum Advantages and Risks

Take the lump sum when: you have investing discipline; you want to leave an inheritance; your health is below average; the pension fund looks shaky (check PBGC coverage); you need the cash for debt or a home.

Take the annuity when: longevity runs in your family; you value guaranteed income over control; you lack investing experience; your spouse needs protected income.

Tax note: Rolling a lump sum directly into an IRA avoids immediate taxation. Annuity payments are taxed as ordinary income as received.

Rollover mechanics can make or break the lump sum. A direct rollover to an IRA triggers $0 in current tax. But a 60-day indirect rollover forces mandatory 20% federal withholding — on $400,000, that's $80,000 withheld, and you must replace that $80,000 from other savings within 60 days or it becomes taxable income (plus a 10% penalty if you're under 59½). A single botched rollover can manufacture an $80,000+ tax bill out of thin air. Always elect the direct trustee-to-trustee transfer and confirm it in writing.

Vet the plan's health before trusting the annuity. The PBGC backstops most private pensions, but guarantees shrink for early retirement — roughly 45% of the age-65 maximum at 55 — and health benefits aren't covered at all. Read your plan's annual funding notice: funding below 80% is a yellow flag worth investigating. Finally, shop the lump sum: get three retail single-premium immediate annuity quotes. Competitive insurers occasionally beat a plan's conversion rate by 5-8%, turning the "lump sum vs annuity" question into "lump sum vs a better annuity."
