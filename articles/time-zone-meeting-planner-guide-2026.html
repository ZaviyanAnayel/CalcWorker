Finding the Overlap Window

Team: New York (ET), London (GMT/BST), Karachi (PKT).

  • NY 9am-5pm ET = 2pm-10pm London = 7pm-3am Karachi
  • Overlap: 2pm-5pm London / 9am-12pm NY / 7pm-10pm Karachi

Rule: Map everyone's 9-to-5 into one reference zone; the intersection is your meeting window. Under 2 hours of overlap means async-first culture.

Four zones collapse the window brutally fast. Take New York (ET), London (GMT/BST), Dubai (GST, UTC+4), Singapore (SGT, UTC+8) and map to UTC: NY 9-5 ET = 13:00-21:00 UTC; London = 09:00-17:00; Dubai = 05:00-13:00; Singapore = 01:00-09:00. The four-way intersection is exactly one hour: 13:00 UTC — 9am New York, 1pm London, 5pm Dubai, 9pm Singapore. Guard that hour fiercely; it's the only slot that asks nobody for midnight. Everything else goes async, no exceptions.

Follow-the-sun turns zones into a feature instead of a tax. New York hands off at 5pm ET (21:00 UTC); Singapore picks up at 9am SGT (01:00 UTC) — an 8-hour code-review or support cycle completes overnight while everyone sleeps. The math: three 8-hour shifts across ET → CET → SGT cover 24 hours with 2-hour handoff overlaps. It works beautifully for support and QA; it fails for creative collaboration, where the same overlap analysis shows you need 2+ shared hours. Use time zones for throughput, overlap for thinking.

DST Traps

  • US DST: Second Sunday March to first Sunday November (2026: Mar 8 - Nov 1).
  • EU DST: Last Sunday March to last Sunday October - 3 weeks of mismatch with the US each year.
  • No DST: Most of Asia, Africa; Arizona, Hawaii.
  • Southern hemisphere: DST is inverted (summer = Dec-Feb).

Fix: Schedule in UTC ("1400 UTC") - unambiguous worldwide. Calendar invites with time zones handle the rest.

2026's DST gaps, precisely. The US springs forward March 8; the EU March 29 — three weeks where New York is UTC-4 but London is still UTC+0 (a 4-hour gap instead of 5). In fall, the EU falls back October 25 while the US waits until November 1 — one week where London is UTC+0 and New York is still UTC-4. A recurring meeting scheduled "9am ET / 2pm London" silently becomes 9am/1pm for a week each fall and 10am/2pm for three weeks each spring. Audit every recurring cross-Atlantic meeting each March and October, or the calendar will embarrass you.

The southern hemisphere inverts everything. Sydney's AEDT (UTC+11) runs October-April — when New York is on standard time (UTC-5), Sydney sits 16 hours ahead, so 9am Monday in Sydney is 5pm Sunday in New York. "Tuesday 10am Sydney / Monday 6pm New York" confuses everyone about which Monday. Then the oddballs: Lord Howe Island shifts by 30 minutes (not an hour) for DST, and Queensland, Western Australia, and the Northern Territory skip DST entirely — Brisbane stays UTC+10 while Sydney jumps to +11, splitting a one-hour domestic gap for half the year.

The UTC Method

UTC = Local Time - UTC Offset

3pm Karachi (UTC+5): 15:00 - 5 = 10:00 UTC.

10:00 UTC in New York (EDT, UTC-4): 10:00 - 4 = 6:00 AM EDT.

Pro tip: keep a world clock with UTC pinned - every conversion becomes one addition/subtraction.

Write it in ISO 8601 and ambiguity dies: 2026-10-14T14:00:00Z means October 14, 2pm UTC, everywhere on Earth — the trailing Z ("Zulu") is the entire time zone. Compare "2pm ET on Oct 14," which a European colleague reads as... their 2pm? Yours? Calendar invites store UTC under the hood and render local time per device, which is why the invite is always right and the email thread is always wrong. For global teams, put the UTC time in the meeting title itself: "Standup 13:00Z" survives every forward and screenshot.

Aviation runs on Zulu for exactly this reason: a flight departing 14:00Z arrives 22:00Z regardless of how many zones it crosses — pilots never convert en route. Apply the same discipline to deadlines: "deploy by 18:00Z Friday" beats "by EOD Friday" across a distributed team (whose EOD?). The conversion chain generalizes from the section: UTC = local − offset, then local₂ = UTC + offset₂. So 10:00Z in Sydney in January (AEDT, UTC+11) is 9pm the same day — meaning a 10am Karachi standup is a 9pm Sydney evening, the whole overlap problem in one line.

Fair Scheduling for Global Teams

  1. Rotate pain: Alternate early/late slots so one region isn't always up at midnight.
  2. Core hours: Define 2-3 overlap hours as sacred meeting time.
  3. Async default: Record meetings; decisions in writing.
  4. No-meeting blocks: Protect focus time across zones.
  5. State zones explicitly: "Thursday 2pm ET / 7pm London / 12am Karachi" - never assume.

A concrete rotation for NY/London/Karachi. Week A: 9am ET / 2pm London / 7pm Karachi — Karachi stays late. Week B: 7am ET / 12pm London / 5pm Karachi — New York takes the early hit. Alternate weekly and publish the rotation a quarter ahead so people plan around their "bad weeks." Nobody suffers more than 50% of the time, and the fairness is the feature: teams that rotate report 30-40% fewer complaints about meeting times than teams with fixed slots that permanently punish one region.

Cap the pain numerically. No one attends more than 2 off-hours meetings (before 8am or after 7pm local) per week — beyond that, cognitive performance and resentment both degrade measurably. Protect one no-meeting day per week per region so deep work survives the calendar. And set an async SLA: decisions posted in writing get 24 hours for same-region and 48 hours for cross-region responses — the SLA replaces the 11pm "quick sync" everyone hates. State every invite in all three zones; ten seconds of typing prevents the #1 cause of missed global meetings.
