The Basic Conversion

Hourly = Annual Salary / 2,080 (40 hrs x 52 weeks)

$75,000 salary: 75,000 / 2,080 = $36.06/hr.

Reverse: $36/hr x 2,080 = $74,880/yr.

Quick mental math: hourly x 2, then add three zeros ($36 x 2 = 72, ~$72K). Slightly under, but close.

The 2,000-hour shortcut works for mental math: $75,000 / 2,000 = $37.50, then shave about 4% for the real 2,080 divisor - roughly $36. Divide by two, drop three zeros, subtract a hair. Accurate enough for comparing offers live on a phone call, when nobody will wait for a calculator.

Reconcile your pay stub: $75,000 / 26 pay periods = $2,884.62 biweekly gross, which must equal hourly x 80 hours. If the stub does not match, something is off - an unpaid holiday, a benefits deduction change, or an error. Catching it in January beats discovering it in December.

Use your true hourly rate to price your time on big decisions. At a $32/hr true rate, a 45-minute-each-way commute costs 1.5 × $32 = $48/day in time — $12,000/year over 250 workdays, before gas. Suddenly a $200/month closer apartment or two remote days a week has a clear ROI. Apply the same math to chores vs outsourcing: if house cleaning costs $25/hr and your true rate is $40/hr, outsourcing buys back profitable hours.

A $75K job at 55 hrs/week pays like $54.5K at 40 hrs. Always divide by actual hours when comparing offers.

Non-exempt status can flip the comparison entirely. At $36.06/hour working 50-hour weeks with time-and-a-half overtime: 40 x 36.06 + 10 x 54.09 = $1,442 + $541 = $1,983/week, about $103K a year. An "hourly" role with real overtime can beat a higher headline salary - always price the expected hours, not the rate alone.

The commute is an unpaid second shift. One hour a day over 250 workdays is 250 hours; $75,000 / 2,330 total hours = $32.19 effective versus $36.06 - an 11% hidden pay cut. A remote role at identical salary is an 11% raise wearing a hoodie, before counting gas and parking.

Adding Benefits Value

  • Health insurance: Employer pays ~$7,000-$22,000/yr (single vs family).
  • 401(k) match: 3-6% of salary ($2,250-$4,500 on $75K).
  • PTO: 15 days = 5.8% of workdays - worth ~$4,350 on $75K.
  • Total comp: $75K salary easily = $90K-$100K+ total value.

Contractors: add 25-40% to salary-equivalent hourly to cover self-employment tax, insurance, and unpaid time off.

Price your PTO in dollars: $75,000 / 260 workdays = $288 per day, so 20 PTO days are worth $5,769. "Unlimited PTO" averages about 10 days actually taken - $2,885 - making it worth roughly half of a fixed 20-day policy. Unlimited sounds generous; the expected value says otherwise.

Compare total comp, not salary lines. Job A: $75K salary + $12K employer family health plan + $3K 401(k) match = $90K. Job B: $80K salary, bare. A wins by $10,000 despite the lower headline number. Build the full ledger - salary, health, retirement, PTO value - before ranking offers.

Negotiation Math

  1. Know your number: Target salary / 2,080 = walk-away hourly.
  2. Counter in ranges: "I'm targeting $78K-$85K" anchors high.
  3. Compare total comp, not salary - a $72K job with great benefits beats $78K bare.
  4. Overtime-exempt? Factor expected hours into the real rate.
  5. Raise math: 3% on $75K = $2,250/yr = $1.08/hr. Small raises compound slowly - job changes (10-20%) move the needle.

Anchor with a range, settle above your target. Asking $85K-$92K and settling at $88K gains $13K over a $75K start - that is $6.25/hour, about $1,083/month gross. The range makes $88K feel like a compromise rather than a demand, and you still land $3K above the range floor. Never open with your walk-away number.

Salary compounds; bonuses do not. A $10K signing bonus is paid once, but a $5K base-salary bump at 3% annual raises is worth about $26,500 over five years (5000 x (1.03^5 - 1)/0.03). Always trade bonus dollars for base dollars in negotiation - the bonus is a gift, the salary is an annuity.
