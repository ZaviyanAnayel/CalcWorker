The Two Main Formulas

Epley: 1RM = Weight x (1 + Reps / 30)
Brzycki: 1RM = Weight / (1.0278 - 0.0278 x Reps)

Example: Bench 225 lb x 8 reps. Epley: 225 x 1.267 = 285 lb; Brzycki: 225 / 0.8054 = 279 lb. Average: about 282 lb.

A third formula for triangulation — Lombardi: 1RM = weight x reps^0.10. Bench 225 x 8: 8^0.10 = e^(0.1 x 2.079) = 1.231 → 277 lb. Now you hold three estimates: Epley 285, Brzycki 279, Lombardi 277. Report the range (277-285) and program off the middle (~281) instead of worshipping one formula's decimal — the spread is the honest answer.

Lower-body example, where formulas diverge: squat 315 x 3. Epley: 315 x 1.1 = 347; Brzycki: 315 / (1.0278 - 0.0834) = 315 / 0.9444 = 334. The 13-lb spread shows formulas disagree more on grinds than on upper-body reps, and deadlifts skew highest of all because lockout strength exceeds rep strength. Never extrapolate a max from beyond 5 reps on the big lifts — test it instead.

Accuracy by Rep Range

Reps UsedEstimate Quality
1-3Excellent (within 2%)

7-10Fair (within 8%)
12+Poor - muscular endurance skews it

Estimates assume a true max-effort set. Leaving "2 in the tank" underestimates 1RM by ~5%. Beginners' estimates run high - neural efficiency improves faster than formulas expect.

Lift-specific bias is real: bench estimates are the most accurate (stable technique, short range of motion); deadlift estimates run 5-10% high because touch-and-go sets hide weak lockouts; overhead press estimates run low because small muscles fatigue faster than the formulas assume. And count only competition-legal reps — a bounced bench rep or hitched deadlift inflates the input, and garbage in means garbage out with interest.

Cross-check with reps-in-reserve: if 225 x 8 felt like RPE 8 (2 reps left), the true 8RM is ~235, making 1RM ≈ 235 x 1.267 = 298, not 285. RPE-anchored estimates beat raw-rep math because they encode effort. Quick scale: RPE 10 = 0 left, RPE 9 = 1 left (~97.5%), RPE 8 = 2 left (~95%) — calibrate your sets and the formulas sharpen immediately.

Training Zones From Your 1RM

  • Strength (1-5 reps): 85-100% of 1RM
  • Hypertrophy (6-12 reps): 65-85%
  • Endurance (12-20 reps): 50-65%
  • Power: 30-60% moved explosively

282 lb bench example: Strength work at 240-282 lb; hypertrophy at 185-240 lb; warm-ups at 140-185 lb.

Programming math off a 281-lb max: 5x5 at 80% = 225 lb; triples at 87% = 245; deload week at 60% = 170; warm-up ladder: bar x 10, 135 x 5, 185 x 3, 225 x 1, 255 x 1, then work sets. Percentages keep every session honest — "heavy day" means 85%+, not "whatever felt heavy." Write the numbers on the program before you touch the bar; deciding loads mid-workout is how "heavy" quietly becomes "medium."

Read the %→reps table backward for AMRAP sets: 100%→1, 95%→2, 90%→3-4, 85%→5-6, 80%→8, 75%→10, 70%→12. Hit 8 reps at 225? Then 225 / 0.80 = 281 max — a built-in estimator you can run mid-workout without a calculator. Lifters who know this table never wonder what to load; they derive it from last week's performance.

Testing Your True 1RM Safely

  1. Only test after 3+ months of consistent training.
  2. Warm up: bar x10, 50% x5, 70% x3, 85% x1, 95% x1.
  3. Rest 3-5 minutes between heavy attempts.
  4. Use a spotter (bench/squat) or safety pins.
  5. Limit to 3 true attempts; stop if form breaks.

Peaking protocol for test day: hit your last heavy single (92-95%) 7-10 days out, then taper volume ~50% while keeping intensity — you arrive fresh, not detrained. Attempt selection: opener 92.5% (a weight you've tripled), second 97.5-100%, third 102.5% only if the second moved fast. Nine-for-nine beats one heroic miss; missed maxes teach your nervous system to fail, and grinders over 10 seconds rarely count anyway.

Don't test while in a hard cut (strength drops 5-10%), sleeping under 6 hours, or nursing any joint pain — estimates from recent training data are safer than a max attempt on a compromised body. Cap true max testing at every 8-12 weeks; between tests, let rep PRs do the talking — a 5-lb improvement on your 5RM implies ~6 lb on the max, no platform required.
