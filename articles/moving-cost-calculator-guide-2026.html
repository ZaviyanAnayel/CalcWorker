Local: ~$100-$150/hr for a crew. Long-distance: ~$0.75-$1.00 per lb, or $4-$7 per mile.

Binding versus non-binding is the money question. A $4,200 non-binding estimate can legally become $6,800 on moving day — under the federal 110% rule you pay 110% at delivery ($4,620 now) and the remaining $2,180 gets invoiced later. A $5,000 binding estimate stays $5,000 unless you add items. The $800 "savings" of the non-binding quote is usually bait; 2026 complaint data consistently shows non-binding finals running 25-40% over estimate. Get binding or binding-not-to-exceed in writing, with the inventory list attached as an exhibit.

Watch 2026's price drivers. Diesel surcharges add 8-15% to long-distance quotes — verify whether the quote includes fuel or tacks it on later. Labor shortages keep summer rates 20-30% above winter. Worked per-mile check: 1,200 miles at $5.50/mile = $6,600 for a 2-bed — at the top of the table's $3,500-$6,000 band, so question anything above $7,000 hard. Always get the quote's weight or cubic-foot basis in writing; "about $5,000" without a stated basis is a guess, not a quote.

Declutter first: Every 1,000 lb shed saves ~$750-$1,000 long-distance. Sell or donate heavy, low-value items.

You hold the legal right to observe the weigh-in and demand a re-weigh — interstate movers must comply under federal rules. The truck weighs full, then empty, and the difference is your shipment; dispute the weight and request the re-weigh before unloading, because after unloading the evidence is gone. Know your heavy hitters: upright piano 500-800 lb ($600-$800 to move), king mattress set ~150 lb, sectional sofa 250-350 lb, treadmill 200-300 lb. Three "keep it" decisions on heavy items can add $1,500+ to a long-distance bill.

The 7-lb-per-cubic-foot rule converts volume estimates: a 1,000 cu ft shipment ≈ 7,000 lb ≈ $5,250-$7,000 at $0.75-$1.00/lb. Movers quoting by cubic feet (common in non-binding quotes) can inflate volume — 1,000 cu ft at $5/cu ft = $5,000 sounds fine until the truck's 1,700 cu ft capacity reveals the padding. Declutter math, extended: selling a $200 treadmill for $100 instead of moving it 1,000 miles ($150-$200 in weight charges) nets you $250-$300 versus keeping it.

Hidden Fees to Watch

  • Stairs/elevator: $50-$150 per flight.
  • Long carry: $100-$200 if the truck can't park close.
  • Packing: $500-$2,000+ if movers pack.
  • Valuation coverage: Basic ($0.60/lb) is nearly worthless; full-value costs 1-2% of declared value.
  • Storage-in-transit: $100-$300/month.
  • Shuttle: $300-$800 if a big truck can't reach your street.

Valuation coverage, decoded. Released value ($0.60/lb) pays $30 for a destroyed 50-lb TV — effectively nothing. Full-value protection at 1-2% of declared value: $50,000 declared × 1.5% = $750, and the mover must repair, replace, or pay full value. Deductible options ($250-$500) cut the premium 20-30%. Third-party moving insurance often beats mover-offered FVP at 1-1.5% with better claims handling. Whatever you choose, photograph everything and declare high-value items ($100+/lb: art, electronics) in writing — undeclared, they're capped at $0.60/lb.

Bulky-item surcharges stack fast: piano $300-$600, pool table $400-$800 (plus $300-$500 re-felting at destination), hot tub $400-$1,000, heavy safe $300-$500. Access fees from the section compound: a 4th-floor walk-up ($150/flight × 3 = $450) plus a long carry ($150) plus a shuttle ($500) adds $1,100 to a "cheap" quote. Ask for the access-fee schedule before booking — reputable movers publish it; movers who "don't know until moving day" are planning the surprise.

Saving Money

  1. Move off-peak: Mid-month, weekdays, October-April - 20-30% cheaper.
  2. DIY hybrid: Pack yourself, hire loaders/unloaders only ($300-$600).
  3. Container moves (PODS): 30-50% less than full-service for flexible timelines.
  4. Get 3+ quotes: Binding estimates protect against final-day surprises.
  5. Tax deduction: Active military moves may still deduct; civilians lost this in 2018 (check current law).

DIY truck math for the 1,000-mile 2-bed: truck rental $1,200-$1,800 + fuel (~$400: 1,000 miles ÷ 10 MPG × $4/gal) + insurance $150 + pizza for friends $100 = ~$1,850-$2,450 versus $3,500-$6,000 full-service — savings of $1,500-$3,500 for 2-3 days of your labor. The hybrid sweet spot: you pack, hired loaders/unloaders ($300-$600 per end) handle the heavy work — total ~$2,500-$3,500 with your back intact. Price the truck 6+ weeks out; one-way rentals spike 30-50% in the final 2 weeks.

Container moves (PODS, U-Pack) run 30-50% under full-service: a 2-bed 1,000-mile move ~$2,500-$3,500 versus $5,000+ full-service, with free storage built in (containers sit at the facility for $150-$250/month versus $300+ for storage-in-transit). You load on your schedule — no mover's 4-hour window. Timing discounts are real: mid-month (5th-25th) weekdays, October-April moves price 20-30% below end-of-month summer — a $6,000 June 30th quote becomes ~$4,500 on October 15th. Book 8 weeks ahead for peak season; 4 suffices off-peak.
