How Harvesting Works

Sell investments at a loss to offset gains (or income), then repurchase similar (not identical) exposure. You keep market position while banking a tax deduction.

Tax Saved = Harvested Loss × Your Marginal Tax Rate

Example: $12,000 harvested loss at 24% bracket = $2,880 tax saved. Losses offset gains dollar-for-dollar first, then up to $3,000/year of ordinary income (remainder carries forward indefinitely).

Direct indexing industrializes harvesting: separately managed accounts systematically realize 40-60 micro-losses a year on a $500,000 portfolio, generating $8,000-$15,000 in annual losses where an index fund generates $0. At 24%, that's $1,900-$3,600/year of "tax alpha" — but advisory fees of 0.25-0.40% ($1,250-$2,000) eat half of it. The net benefit is real but much thinner than marketing suggests; DIY harvesting of your 3-5 biggest losers each December captures most of the value for free.

Character matters more than totals. Short-term losses offset short-term gains first (taxed up to 37%); long-term losses offset long-term gains (15-20%). A $10,000 short-term loss at 35% saves $3,500; a $10,000 long-term loss at 15% saves $1,500. Harvest with intent: prioritize realizing short-term losses (highest value) and think twice before burning long-term losses against the $3,000 ordinary-income cap — save those for future gains instead.

The Wash Sale Rule

Sell at a loss and buy a "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after → loss disallowed.

  • Violates: Sell VTI at a loss, buy VTI 10 days later.
  • OK: Sell VTI at a loss, buy SCHB (different index fund, similar exposure).
  • Applies across accounts: Including IRAs — selling in taxable and rebuying in your IRA triggers it.
  • Crypto exception: Wash sale rules currently do not apply to crypto (proposed legislation may change this).

Map the 61-day window precisely: 30 days before + the sale date + 30 days after. Sell at a loss on December 15 and any purchase from November 15 through January 14 triggers the wash sale — including automatic dividend reinvestments and 401(k) contributions into the same fund. Turn off auto-reinvestment 31 days before you harvest, and check every account (taxable, IRA, 401(k), spouse's accounts) for overlapping purchases. Most accidental wash sales come from a $200 dividend reinvestment, not a deliberate repurchase.

The IRA trap is permanent: sell VTI at a loss in taxable, rebuy it in your IRA within 30 days, and the loss is disallowed forever — IRAs get no basis adjustment to preserve it. A $5,000 loss × 24% = $1,200 vaporized permanently. Your spouse's accounts count too. Crypto remains exempt from wash-sale rules for now — harvest freely there, but expect Congress to close that gap eventually.

Year-End Harvesting Playbook

  1. November review: Scan taxable accounts for unrealized losses.
  2. Match against gains: Prioritize offsetting short-term gains (taxed highest).
  3. Swap, don't sit out: Replace with a similar-but-not-identical fund immediately.
  4. Mind the $3,000 cap: Harvesting $50K of losses with no gains banks $3K/year deductions for 16+ years via carryforward.
  5. December 31 deadline: Trades must settle by year-end (trade date controls).

T+1 settlement changed the year-end calendar: a trade executed December 31, 2026 settles January 2, 2027 but still counts for 2026 — the trade date controls, not settlement. Don't cut it that close anyway: year-end volatility and thin holiday volume can move prices against you between decision and execution. Run the harvest scan in November, execute by mid-December, and treat the final week as contingency only.

Dodge the mutual-fund distribution trap: buy a fund on December 10 and it distributes $2/share on December 15 — you owe tax on the full distribution despite owning the fund for five days. It's phantom income on money that was already yours. Check ex-distribution dates before any December mutual-fund purchase; when in doubt, wait until January. ETFs largely avoid this, but mutual funds distribute annually like clockwork.

When Harvesting Backfires

  • Low bracket: At 0% capital gains rates, harvesting wastes losses you could use later at higher rates.
  • Short-term thinking: Harvesting resets your holding period — a new purchase starts the 1-year long-term clock over.
  • Transaction costs: Spreads and fees on small positions can exceed tax savings.
  • State taxes: Some states (looking at you, New Jersey) don't allow capital loss carryforwards.

Bracket waste is harvesting's silent killer. Realizing $30,000 of losses in a 12% year captures just $3,600 of value — and against the $3,000/year ordinary-income cap, only $360/year. Banked for a 24-32% year, the same losses are worth $7,200-$9,600. Worse: unused carryforwards die with you — heirs get the stepped-up basis but not your $40,000 loss carryforward. Harvest when rates are high, use carryforwards aggressively, and never let a large stockpile sit idle into old age.

States don't all conform. New Jersey doesn't allow capital-loss carryforwards at all — harvest there and the state benefit is use-it-this-year-or-never. Pennsylvania is similarly restrictive. A harvest that saves $2,880 federally might save $0 at the state level while resetting your holding period. Model federal and state before executing; the federal tail shouldn't wag the state dog, especially in nonconforming states.
