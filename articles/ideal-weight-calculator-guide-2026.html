The Classic Formulas

Devine (men): 50 kg + 2.3 kg per inch over 5 ft
Devine (women): 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg per inch over 5 ft

5'10" man: 50 + 2.3x10 = 73 kg = 161 lb. 5'5" woman: 45.5 + 2.3x5 = 57 kg = 126 lb.

Robinson and Miller formulas adjust slightly; Hamwi (used clinically for drug dosing) gives similar results. All were designed for medication dosing, not aesthetics - treat them as reference points.

Formula comparison for a 5'10" man — Devine: 161 lb; Robinson (52 + 1.9 x 10 = 71 kg): 156 lb; Miller (56.2 + 1.41 x 10 = 70.3 kg): 155 lb; Hamwi (106 + 6 x 10): 166 lb. An 11-lb spread from formulas answering the same question — because they were built for drug dosing, where "close enough" is calibrated differently than aesthetics. Treat any single output as ±5 lb and you'll never be misled by false precision.

The clinical use case, since it's the real one: chemotherapy and anesthesia dose from ideal or adjusted body weight, not actual — for obese patients, adjusted = IBW + 0.4 x (actual - IBW). A 250-lb man with IBW 161: adjusted = 161 + 0.4 x 89 = 197 lb dosing weight. This is the formulas' actual job; your mirror was never the intended audience.

Frame Size Adjustments

Measure wrist circumference to gauge frame:

FrameMen (wrist)Women (wrist)Adjustment
SmallUnder 6.5"Under 5.75"-10%
Medium6.5-7.5"5.75-6.25"Base
LargeOver 7.5"Over 6.25"+10%

Large-framed 5'10" man: 161 x 1.10 = 177 lb - a 16-lb swing from frame alone.

Elbow breadth is the alternative frame test: extend the arm, measure across the elbow — men over 2.75", women over 2.5" suggest large frames. Or the height/wrist ratio: height ÷ wrist in inches — men >10.4 = small, 9.6-10.4 = medium, <9.6 = large (women: >11 = small, 10.1-11 = medium, <10.1 = large). A 5'10" (70") man with a 6.5" wrist: ratio 10.8 = small frame — his 161-lb Devine target stands unadjusted, no +10% for him.

Muscle overrides frame: a lifter with 2+ years of training adds 10-15% over formula weight — our large-framed 177-lb example becomes ~195-200 lb lean. Cross-check with FFMI: if the formula weight implies FFMI under 20 for a trained man, the formula is underweighting his muscle. Formulas describe skeletons; training builds the house, and the house weighs more.

BMI Range: The Saner Target

Convert the healthy BMI range (18.5-24.9) to weight for your height:

Weight Range = BMI x Height(m) squared

5'10" (1.78m): 18.5 x 3.17 = 129 lb to 24.9 x 3.17 = 174 lb. Range: 129-174 lb.

Ranges beat single numbers - healthy weight varies with muscle, age, and build. Aim for the range, then refine by waist, body fat, and how you feel.

Second height worked: 5'5" (1.65 m, 2.7225 m²): 18.5 x 2.7225 = 50.4 kg = 111 lb; 24.9 x 2.7225 = 67.8 kg = 149 lb. Range: 111-149 lb — a 38-lb span that embarrasses single-number formulas. Note the bottom: 111 lb at 5'5" suits almost no adult woman with muscle; most feel and perform best in the upper-middle of their range, which is exactly what ranges (not points) are for.

The mortality U-curve: large cohorts find lowest all-cause mortality around BMI 22-25, rising below 20 and above 27-30 — but the curve shifts right with muscle and age. Athletes sit healthily at 26-27; adults over 65 at 24-28. "Ideal" is a range with a personal sweet spot, found via waist, energy, bloodwork, and strength — not via a formula's decimal.

Setting a Realistic Goal Weight

  1. Start from your BMI range, adjusted for frame and muscle.
  2. Consider your weight history - maintaining near your adult baseline is easier than chasing high-school weight.
  3. Set process goals (training, protein, sleep) alongside outcome goals.
  4. Reassess every 10 lb - body composition changes the target.
  5. Talk to your doctor about medications or conditions affecting weight.

Respect the set point: the body defends a ~10-15 lb range via hunger hormones — a 220-lb adult targeting 150 fights biology the whole way, while targeting 198-208 (5-10% loss) works with it and still transforms health markers (diabetes risk −50%+ at 7% loss in the DPP trial). Stage the goal: lose 10%, stabilize 3 months, then decide if another 10% is worth it. Staircases beat cliffs.

Timeline honesty: 1 lb/week = 20 lb in 20 weeks, plus 2 diet breaks = ~26 weeks — six months, not six weeks. SMART-framed: "Reach 195 lb by April (0.8 lb/week) via 2,200 cal/day, 4 lifts/week, 8k steps" beats "lose weight" the way a map beats a wish. Reassess every 10 lb: body composition, hunger, and labs may move the target — the goal weight is a hypothesis, not a contract.
