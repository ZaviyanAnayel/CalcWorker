PMI Rules: 80%, 78%, and the Fine Print

Private mortgage insurance protects the lender, not you — and you pay for it. Federal law (Homeowners Protection Act) sets the removal rules for most conventional loans:

  • Borrower-initiated (80% LTV): Once your balance hits 80% of the original value, you can request cancellation in writing. You must be current on payments with no 30-day lates in the past 12 months.
  • Automatic (78% LTV): The servicer must drop PMI when the balance reaches 78% of original value on the amortization schedule — no request needed, but only if you are current.
  • Final termination: At the loan's midpoint (year 15 of a 30-year loan), PMI must end regardless.
Current LTV = Current Loan Balance ÷ Original Purchase Price (or appraised value)

Know which loans the Homeowners Protection Act does not cover, because the 80%/78% rules will not save you there. FHA loans carry MIP, not PMI - with less than 10% down, MIP lasts the entire loan life regardless of equity, and even with 10%+ down it runs 11 years. VA and USDA loans have guarantee fees, not cancellable PMI. Lender-paid PMI (where you took a higher rate instead) cannot be "removed" at all - your only exit is refinancing. And piggyback second mortgages are separate loans, not insurance. Before strategizing PMI removal, confirm which product you actually hold.

"Original value" has a precise legal meaning that can work against you: it is the lesser of the purchase price or the appraised value at origination. If you bought for $400,000 but the appraisal came in at $390,000, your 80% threshold is $312,000 - based on $390,000, not your purchase price. Low appraisals at purchase quietly raise the bar for every future removal calculation. Pull your original closing documents and use the correct baseline before requesting cancellation, or the servicer will reject your math.

The Appraisal Shortcut: Removing PMI in 2-3 Years

You do not have to wait for the amortization schedule. If your home appreciated, a new appraisal can prove 80% LTV years early.

Example: Bought at $400,000 with 10% down ($360,000 loan, PMI $113/month). After 3 years, balance is about $345,000 — but the home appraises at $440,000. New LTV = $345,000 ÷ $440,000 = 78.4% → PMI cancelled.

Cost: appraisal $300-$600. Payback: $113/month saved → pays for itself in under 6 months. Requirements: typically 2 years of on-time payments (some servicers allow 1 year with significant improvements).

Ask your servicer whether a Broker Price Opinion (BPO) suffices before ordering a $500 appraisal. Many servicers accept a BPO - essentially a realtor's valuation at $150-$250 - for PMI removal when the loan is seasoned and your payment history is clean. The payback math improves dramatically: $113/month in PMI savings against a $175 BPO pays for itself in under two months. Servicers rarely volunteer this option; the script is "do you accept a BPO or exterior-only appraisal for PMI termination, and what is your minimum seasoning requirement?" Get the answer in writing.

Documented improvements can shortcut the seasoning clock. Most servicers require two years of payments before a value-based removal - but several waive it to as little as one year when you can document substantial improvements like a $25,000 kitchen remodel or finished basement with permits and before/after photos. The logic: improvements, not market froth, created the equity. Keep every receipt, permit, and contractor invoice from day one of ownership. A well-documented $30,000 renovation has removed PMI for borrowers in month 14.

How Much PMI Costs You Over Time

PMI is not a small fee. On a $380,000 loan (5% down on $400K), PMI at 0.5%/year costs $158/month.

  • Paid for 7 years (amortization path): $13,272 total
  • Removed after 3 years via appraisal: $5,688 total
  • Savings from acting early: $7,584

FHA loans are worse: with less than 10% down, MIP lasts the entire loan life. The only exits are refinancing into conventional or paying down to 78% and refinancing. If you have an FHA loan and 20% equity, refinancing out is usually the single highest-ROI financial move available.

The FHA-to-conventional refinance is the highest-ROI move in this entire topic. Consider a $380,000 FHA loan at 6.5% with $225/month MIP that lasts for life. Refinancing into a conventional loan at 6.75% - a higher rate - with 20%+ equity eliminates the MIP entirely. Monthly P&I rises about $38, but total monthly cost falls roughly $187. Even after $4,500 in refinance closing costs, payback is about 24 months, and every month after is pure savings. Any FHA borrower who has crossed 20% equity and has not priced this refinance is likely losing hundreds monthly.

Single-premium PMI is the alternative nobody quotes you. Instead of $158/month, you pay roughly 1.5-2.0% of the loan upfront - about $5,700-$7,600 on $380,000 - often financeable with seller concessions or lender credits. Breakeven against monthly PMI lands around 36-48 months. It wins for buyers who will hold 4+ years and can fund it through concessions; it loses if you refinance or hit 20% equity quickly. Ask your lender for a side-by-side: monthly vs. single-premium vs. lender-paid, all three on one page.

Step-by-Step PMI Cancellation Process

  1. Check your balance against 80% of original value (or get an appraisal for current value).
  2. Confirm payment history: no 30-day late payments in the last 12 months, none 60-day late in 24 months.
  3. Submit a written request to your servicer — phone calls alone may not trigger the process.
  4. Pay for the appraisal if required ($300-$600); the servicer orders it.
  5. Get written confirmation of the cancellation date and verify your next statement.

Servicers are legally required to inform you annually of your PMI rights. If yours stonewalls, file a CFPB complaint — it works.

Use a precise script and paper trail with your servicer. Call and say: "I am requesting PMI termination under the Homeowners Protection Act; my loan number is X and my balance is below 80% of original value." Get a reference number, the representative's name, and ask for the request confirmation in writing - then follow up with a written request via the servicer's secure portal or certified mail. Servicers must respond within 30 days. Log every interaction with dates. This sounds bureaucratic because it is: PMI termination disputes are won on documentation, and verbal requests have a way of never having happened.

Expect an escrow re-analysis after PMI drops, and check the math. Removing $158/month in PMI should cut your total payment by at least that much - but servicers simultaneously re-analyze escrow for taxes and insurance, and a tax increase can mask the savings. Review the new escrow statement line by line: some borrowers discover their "PMI removal" barely changed the payment because property taxes rose $140/month the same quarter. If escrow looks wrong, you have the right to request the analysis worksheet and dispute errors within 30 days.
