The Cost Per Hire Formula

CPH = (External Costs + Internal Costs) / Total Hires

Example company (25 hires/year):

  • External: $60K (job ads, agency fees, background checks, relocation)
  • Internal: $50K (recruiter salaries portion, hiring manager interview time, HR admin)
  • CPH: $110,000 / 25 = $4,400

SHRM benchmark: ~$4,700 average. Executive/agency hires run $15K-$30K+ each.

Break CPH by source and the averages lie. Job-board postings ($300-$600/post) typically yield $500-$1,200 cost per hire; agencies charge 20-25% of first-year salary ($15K-$25K on an $80K hire); RPO contracts run $8K-$15K per hire at volume. A company making 25 hires at $4,400 blended CPH might be spending $1,000 each on 20 job-board hires and $22,000 each on 5 agency hires — the blended number hides that the agency channel costs 22x more. Track CPH per source, not just overall, or you'll never see where the money burns.

Company size skews the benchmark hard. SHRM's ~$4,700 average blends enterprises with dedicated TA teams (often $3,000-$4,000 CPH at scale) against small firms making 5-10 hires a year ($6,000-$9,000 CPH — fixed costs spread thin). Executive search is a different sport entirely: $30K-$75K+ per placement at 30-35% of first-year cash comp. And if your CPH beats $4,700 but time-to-fill runs 60+ days, you're optimizing the wrong metric — vacancy cost dwarfs hiring cost by an order of magnitude.

Hidden Internal Costs

  • Hiring manager time: 30 hrs per hire x $75/hr loaded = $2,250 often uncounted.
  • Team interviews: 5 people x 3 hrs x $60 = $900 per hire.
  • Productivity drag: Open roles burden the team for 44 days average (time-to-fill).
  • Bad hires: A failed hire costs 30% of salary minimum - quality beats speed.

A typical interview loop burns more hours than anyone admits. Six interviewers × 1-hour interviews + 30 minutes prep/debrief each = 9 hours; the hiring manager adds 8-10 hours of sourcing review and deliberation; the recruiter 12-15 hours of screening and coordination. At a blended $80/hour loaded rate, that's $2,320-$2,720 of labor per hire — before the offer stage. Structured scorecards cut this ~30%: fewer, sharper interviews (4 instead of 6) save roughly $900/hire with better predictive validity than the bloated panel.

Assessment and screening tools stack up invisibly. Skills tests at $50-$200 per candidate × 20 assessed candidates = $1,000-$4,000 per hire; background checks $75-$200; drug screens $30-$75; reference-check services $100-$300. For high-volume roles these per-candidate costs dominate — 200 applicants × a $75 assessment = $15,000 for a single hire if you test too early in the funnel. Sequence cheap filters first (resume screen, 15-minute phone screen) and reserve paid assessments for finalists only.

Reducing Cost Per Hire

  1. Employee referrals: $1K-$3K bonuses beat $15K agency fees; referrals also retain 45% longer.
  2. Talent pipeline: Evergreen postings and community building cut time-to-fill.
  3. Structured interviews: Fewer, better interviews - cut panel bloat.
  4. Employer brand: Inbound applicants cost ~50% less than sourced ones.
  5. Internal mobility: Promotions cost a fraction of external hires.

Referral economics are nearly unbeatable. A $2,000-$3,000 referral bonus versus a $15,000-$20,000 agency fee saves $12K-$18K per hire — and referred employees stay 45% longer (cutting future CPH too) and ramp ~20% faster. A 50-hire company shifting 30% of hires from agencies to referrals saves roughly 15 × $14,000 = $210,000/year. Boost it structurally: pay half the bonus at hire and half at 6 months, and add $500 premiums for referrals in underrepresented roles.

AI sourcing tools compress the top of the funnel: a $400-$600/month sourcing platform replacing 10 hours/week of manual LinkedIn sourcing at a $50/hour recruiter cost saves ~$1,500/month net while doubling qualified pipeline. Evergreen postings ("always hiring" for recurring roles) build a warm bench that cuts time-to-fill 30-40% — the posting costs $300 once and yields candidates for a year. Internal mobility remains the cheapest channel: a promotion costs ~$1,000-$2,000 in process costs versus $4,400+ external, with near-zero ramp risk.

Time-to-Fill Matters Too

Vacancy Cost = Daily Revenue Impact x Days Open

Sales role generating $2K/day, open 44 days: $88,000 in lost revenue - dwarfing the $4,400 hiring cost.

Optimize for quality + speed: a fast bad hire is the most expensive outcome. Track quality-of-hire (performance ratings, retention) alongside cost.

Benchmark time-to-fill by role or the average misleads. Software engineering: 50-60 days. Sales: 30-40. Customer support: 20-30. Executive: 90-120. Cutting engineering time-to-fill from 60 to 40 days at $2,000/day vacancy impact saves $40,000 per hire — ten times the $4,400 CPH. The biggest lever is decision speed: top candidates are off the market in about 10 days, yet average offer processes drag 3-4 weeks. Every week of delay past day 14 loses roughly 15% of your remaining finalist slate.

Quota-carrying vacancies bleed hardest. A $600K-quota rep vacant 60 days misses ~$100K of prorated quota ($600K ÷ 365 × 60) — and the replacement ramps over 3-6 months, so the true revenue hole is $150K-$200K. That's 35-45x the $4,400 hiring cost. For these roles, optimize speed and quality simultaneously: pre-approved comp bands (offer within 48 hours), dedicated sourcing, and a paid contractor bridge ($8K-$12K for the gap) that pays for itself if it preserves even 10% of the quota.
