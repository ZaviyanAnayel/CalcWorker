Runway Formula

Runway (months) = Cash in Bank / Monthly Net Burn
Net Burn = Monthly Expenses - Monthly Revenue

$2M seed, $150K/mo expenses, $40K/mo revenue: Net burn = $110K. Runway = 2,000,000 / 110,000 = 18.2 months.

Gross burn ($150K) measures spending pace; net burn ($110K) measures cash drain. Runway uses net.

"$2M in the bank" is rarely $2M you can spend. Security deposits, payroll-tax reserves, and restricted grant funds routinely lock up 10-20% of the headline balance. Worked example: $2M raised, minus a $180K payroll reserve and a $120K office deposit, leaves $1.7M of usable cash. At $110K monthly net burn, true runway is 15.5 months — not the 18.2 the bank balance suggests. Also exclude committed-but-unwired capital: a signed SAFE that hasn't funded is hope, not cash. Always run the formula on usable cash, and reconcile it monthly against the actual bank ledger.

Below 6 months of runway, monthly math is too slow — switch to weekly cash tracking. Example: $660K cash at $110K/month burn reads as 6 months, but a single $90K quarterly AWS prepay or a payroll-timing quirk can silently erase 3 weeks. Weekly burn = monthly burn ÷ 4.33 ($25,400 here); flag any week where actual spend beats it by 15%+. Founders who track weekly spot the drift 4-6 weeks earlier than monthly trackers, which is the difference between a calm bridge round and a fire sale.

The Fundraising Clock

Runway LeftAction
18+ monthsBuild; fundraise from strength
12 monthsStart fundraising process
6 monthsUrgent - cut burn, bridge options
3 monthsEmergency - layoffs, acqui-hire talks

Fundraising takes 3-6 months. Starting at 12 months means closing with 6-9 left - the minimum for leverage. Starting at 6 months means desperation pricing.

When the clock hits 6-9 months, a bridge extension usually beats a full priced round. Typical bridge: $250K-$750K on a SAFE with a 20% discount and a valuation cap matching your last round — at $110K burn, $500K buys ~4.5 months and closes in 2-4 weeks with existing investors, versus 3-6 months for a priced round. The catch: bridges stack. Two bridges signal distress to new investors, and the discount compounds dilution — a 20% discount on a $10M cap prices the bridge at an ~$8M effective valuation. Use one bridge as a tool; two as a warning.

Venture debt is the other lever: lenders advance $500K-$2M (roughly 25-35% of your last equity round) at 8-12% interest plus 1-2% warrant coverage, repaid over 24-36 months. It adds 6-12 months of runway without pricing a down round, but covenants can force early repayment if cash dips below a floor — typically 3-6 months of burn. Only take venture debt with 12+ months of runway remaining. At 6 months, the covenants own you, and a tripped covenant can trigger the crisis the debt was meant to prevent.

Extending Runway

Cut $30K/mo from the example: New burn $80K, runway = 2M/80K = 25 months (+7 months).

  1. Hiring freeze - payroll is 70-80% of burn for most startups.
  2. Cut tools/subscriptions - audit every SaaS seat.
  3. Renegotiate - office, vendors, contractors.
  4. Grow revenue - $40K to $70K/mo revenue cuts burn to $80K without any cuts.
  5. Founder pay cuts - symbolic and real.

Revenue beats cuts: growing out of the burn preserves the team that creates value.

Founder pay cuts move the needle more than any SaaS audit. Three founders dropping from $15K to $6K/month saves $27K/month — on the $2M / $110K-burn example, runway stretches from 18.2 to 24.1 months ($2M ÷ $83K), a gain of nearly 6 months from one decision. Pair it with a hiring freeze and the combined $40K-$50K/month reduction pushes an 18-month runway past 26 months. Investors read founder cuts as alignment; they read contractor cuts as panic — sequence the announcement accordingly, founders first.

Revenue-side levers beat cuts dollar-for-dollar because they compound. Offer annual prepay at 15-20% off: 50 customers prepaying $9.6K/year instead of $1K/month pulls $480K of cash forward within 60 days, cutting net burn from $110K toward ~$70K for the year. That single move stretches runway to ~28.6 months ($2M ÷ $70K) — an 8-month gain with zero layoffs. Annual plans also cut churn 30-50% since the renewal decision happens once a year, so the runway gain is structural rather than one-time.

Burn Multiples and Efficiency

Burn Multiple = Net Burn / Net New ARR

Example: $110K/mo burn ($1.32M/yr), adding $600K ARR/yr: burn multiple = 2.2x.

  • Under 1x: excellent efficiency
  • 1-2x: acceptable growth-stage
  • 3x+: burning too much per dollar of growth - fix or perish

Investors in 2026 prize efficient growth over growth-at-all-costs - the burn multiple is the metric they quote.

Pair the burn multiple with the Rule of 40 (growth rate + profit margin ≥ 40%) for the full efficiency picture. A startup growing 60% with a 2.2x burn multiple passes the growth half but flirts with the margin half. In 2026, Series A investors typically demand burn multiples under 1.5x and Rule of 40 scores above 50 for premium valuations; multiples above 2x get funded only with 100%+ growth attached. Track both monthly — a multiple improving from 3x to 1.4x over two quarters is the single strongest fundraise narrative a founder can bring to a pitch.

Worked improvement path: cut net burn from $110K to $80K while holding $600K/year of net new ARR ($50K/month) — the multiple drops from 2.2x to 1.6x ($80K ÷ $50K). Then push net new ARR to $75K/month at the same burn and you're at 1.07x — excellent. The fastest lever is usually pricing: a 10% price increase on $40K MRR adds $4K/month of efficiency without a dollar of extra spend, shaving roughly 0.1x off the multiple every month it holds. Efficiency compounds just like growth does.
