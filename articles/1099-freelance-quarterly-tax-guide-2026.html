Foundations: Net Profit, SE Tax & Federal Brackets

Freelancers file Schedule C to report business income and expenses. The resulting net profit is the starting point for both self‑employment (SE) tax and ordinary income tax.

Net Profit = Gross Income – Business Expenses

Because the IRS treats only 92.35% of net profit as subject to SE tax, the calculation adds a second layer.

Adjusted Net = Net Profit × 0.9235

The SE tax rate for 2026 remains 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare). The formula is:

SE Tax = Adjusted Net × 15.3%

After the SE tax deduction (half of SE tax), the remaining profit is taxed at the ordinary federal brackets. For 2026 the brackets for single filers are:

  • 10% on the first $11,600
  • 12% on $11,601 – $47,300
  • 22% on $47,301 – $100,150
  • 24% on $100,151 – $191,950
  • 32% on $191,951 – $243,350
  • 35% on $243,351 – $609,350
  • 37% on income over $609,350

Quarterly estimated payments use Form 1040‑ES and must cover both the SE tax and the projected income tax.

2026 Worked Example: $85,000 Gross Income

Step 1 – Gather Income & Expenses

Gross freelance earnings: $85,000
Qualified business expenses (software, home‑office, travel): $22,500

Step 2 – Compute Net Profit

Net Profit = $85,000 – $22,500 = $62,500

Step 3 – Adjust for SE Tax Base

Adjusted Net = $62,500 × 0.9235 = $57,734

Step 4 – Calculate SE Tax

SE Tax = $57,734 × 15.3% = $8,839

Step 5 – Deduct Half of SE Tax

Deductible Portion = $8,839 ÷ 2 = $4,420

Step 6 – Determine Taxable Income for Federal Tax

Taxable Income = $62,500 – $4,420 = $58,080

Step 7 – Apply 2026 Federal Brackets

  • 10% on first $11,600 = $1,160
  • 12% on next $35,700 ($47,300‑$11,600) = $4,284
  • 22% on remaining $10,780 ($58,080‑$47,300) = $2,371
Estimated Federal Tax = $1,160 + $4,284 + $2,371 = $7,815 ≈ $7,560 (rounded for quarterly estimate)

Step 8 – Quarterly Payment

Total Annual Tax = SE Tax + Federal Tax = $8,839 + $7,560 = $16,399
Quarterly Estimated Payment = $16,399 ÷ 4 = $4,100 (rounded)

Step 9 – File Form 1040‑ES

Submit $4,100 by each deadline: April 15, June 17, September 16, and January 15 2027. Use the payment voucher included with the 2026 Form 1040‑ES.

Optimization Strategies for Lower Quarterly Burden

  1. Maximize Deductible Expenses: Track every home‑office square foot, mileage, and subscription. Section 179 expensing lets you write off equipment up to $1,160,000 in 2026.
  2. Contribute to a Solo 401(k) or SEP‑IRA: Contributions reduce net profit before SE tax. For 2026, you can defer up to $66,000 (including catch‑up) if age 50+.
  3. Elect the Qualified Business Income (QBI) Deduction: Up to 20% of qualified net profit may be excluded from ordinary income tax, lowering the federal bracket impact.
  4. Safe Harbor Payments: Pay the lesser of 100% of prior‑year tax or 90% of current‑year projected tax to avoid penalties. For new freelancers, 100% of the prior year (often zero) is safe, but the 90% rule protects against underpayment.
  5. Quarterly Review & Adjust: Re‑run the 1099 Freelance Tax Calculator after each major contract. Small changes in income can shift you into a higher bracket, prompting a payment tweak.

Implementing these tactics not only reduces the cash outflow each quarter but also positions you for a smoother year‑end tax filing.
