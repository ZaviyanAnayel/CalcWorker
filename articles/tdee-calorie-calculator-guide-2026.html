BMR: Your Baseline Burn

Mifflin-St Jeor (Men): 10 x kg + 6.25 x cm - 5 x age + 5
Mifflin-St Jeor (Women): 10 x kg + 6.25 x cm - 5 x age - 161

Example: 30-year-old man, 180 lb (81.6 kg), 5'10" (178 cm): BMR = 816 + 1112 - 150 + 5 = 1,783 cal/day - what he burns lying still all day.

Work the women's formula too: 32 years, 145 lb (65.8 kg), 5'5" (165 cm): 10 x 65.8 = 658; 6.25 x 165 = 1031; minus 5 x 32 = 160; minus 161. Total: 658 + 1031 - 160 - 161 = 1,368 cal/day. Cross-check with Katch-McArdle (BMR = 370 + 21.6 x lean mass in kg): at 28% body fat her lean mass is 47.4 kg, giving 370 + 1024 = 1,394 — within 26 calories of Mifflin-St Jeor, which is why both formulas stay in use.

Aging's toll is specific: our 180-lb man at 50 computes 816 + 1112 - 250 + 5 = 1,683 vs 1,783 at 30 — a 100-cal/day decline per two decades, roughly one cookie's worth of "I used to eat whatever I wanted." One semantic trap: most calculators labeled "BMR" actually estimate RMR (resting, not basal), which runs ~10% higher — a 150-cal naming gap that explains why two calculators disagree.

Most people overestimate activity - desk workers who lift 3x/week are "lightly active," not "very active." When in doubt, pick one level lower and adjust after 3 weeks of tracking weight.

Translate real life honestly: 10,000 daily steps ≈ +300-400 cal ≈ 0.15-0.20 on the multiplier; a nurse on 12-hour shifts can legitimately claim 1.725; a programmer who lifts 3x/week is 1.375, full stop. The empirical test beats the label: if your weight holds steady for 3 weeks at the computed TDEE, the multiplier was right regardless of what you called your lifestyle.

The classic double-count: logging a 400-cal run in an app while using the 1.55 multiplier counts it twice. Pick one system — multiplier or sedentary x 1.2 plus logged exercise, never both. After 3 weeks, calibrate: losing 0.5 lb/week less than planned means true TDEE is ~250 cal lower than estimated, so cut intake 250 or add ~3,500 daily steps.

Cutting and Bulking Math

  • Fat loss: TDEE minus 500 cal/day = about 1 lb/week loss. Never drop below BMR for long.
  • Muscle gain: TDEE plus 250-300 cal/day (lean bulk minimizes fat gain).
  • Protein: 0.7-1.0g per lb of body weight daily - 180 lb means 130-180g protein.

Example cut: TDEE 2,764, target 2,264 cal/day. Expected: ~1 lb/week. After 12 weeks: ~12 lb lost. Then recalculate - lighter bodies burn less.

Diet breaks are planned, not failures: after 8-12 weeks of cutting, spend 10-14 days at maintenance TDEE. Leptin and thyroid output partially recover, gym performance rebounds, and adherence data favors planned breaks over white-knuckling into a binge. Expect 2-4 lb of water and glycogen regain — not fat — and resume the deficit after. Two steps forward, one planned half-step back still finishes the staircase.

Lean-bulk composition: +250 cal/day ≈ +0.5 lb/week, of which trained lifters gain roughly 50-70% as muscle (beginners do better). The waist is the governor: gaining over 0.5"/month means the surplus is too big — trim 100-150 cal. After 4-6 months of bulking, a 4-6 week mini-cut at -500 cal strips 4-6 lb of fat without losing the new muscle, then the bulk resumes.

Why the Scale Lies (and What to Track)

  1. Weigh daily, compare weekly averages - water swings 3 lb daily.
  2. Track waist measurement alongside weight.
  3. Give any calorie target 3 full weeks before adjusting.
  4. NEAT (steps, fidgeting) varies hugely - 8,000 vs 3,000 daily steps can differ 300+ calories.
  5. Metabolic adaptation: prolonged dieting drops TDEE 5-15% - diet breaks help.

Quantify the noise so it stops scaring you: 400g of stored carbs bind ~1,200-1,600g of water (3-4g per gram of glycogen), so a big pasta night adds 3-4 lb overnight; creatine loading adds 2-4 lb of intracellular water in week one; the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle adds 2-5 lb. None of it is fat. Judge only 4-week trends — daily readings are weather, the monthly average is climate.

Run the trend method: weigh daily, average Sunday-to-Saturday, compare week 4 to week 1. Example: week-1 average 182.4, week-4 average 179.8 = 2.6 lb lost ≈ 0.65 lb/week, implying a true ~325 cal/day deficit rather than the planned 500. Close the gap by cutting 175 cal or adding 3,500 steps/day — adjust the plan to the data, not the data to the plan.
