Calculating Lean Body Mass

LBM = Body Weight x (1 - Body Fat %)

Example: 200 lb at 20% body fat: LBM = 200 x 0.80 = 160 lb (40 lb fat mass).

The Boer formula estimates LBM from height/weight without body fat %:

Men: 0.407 x kg + 0.267 x cm - 19.2 | Women: 0.252 x kg + 0.473 x cm - 48.3

Boer worked — 200 lb (90.7 kg), 6'0" (183 cm) man: 0.407 x 90.7 = 36.9; 0.267 x 183 = 48.9; sum − 19.2 = 66.6 kg = 147 lb LBM. Compare the simple method at 20% body fat: 160 lb. The 13-lb gap shows Boer assumes average muscularity — it underestimates lifters and overestimates the detrained. Use Boer when you lack a body-fat reading; use weight x (1 − BF%) when you have one; trust neither past the second decimal.

The formula family: Hume (1966) adds age terms and suits clinical estimates; James (1976) was built for drug dosing in the obese. All three agree within ~5 lb for average builds and diverge at extremes — which is exactly when you should stop estimating and get a DEXA. Formulas are placeholders you use while waiting; scans are answers.

FFMI: The Muscle-Building Benchmark

FFMI = LBM (kg) / Height (m) squared (+ normalized for height)
FFMIInterpretation (men)
18-20Average trained
22-23Notable muscularity
25+Near natural limit

Example: 160 lb LBM (72.6 kg) at 5'10" (1.78m): FFMI = 72.6 / 3.17 = 22.9 - well-developed natural physique. (Women: subtract ~2-3 for equivalent categories.)

Height normalization matters: raw FFMI favors the tall. Normalized FFMI = raw + 6.1 x (1.8 − height in meters). A 6'3" (1.905 m) lifter at raw 24: 24 + 6.1 x (−0.105) = 23.4 — still impressive, honestly scaled. Below 5'8", normalization adds points; comparing unnormalized FFMIs across heights is comparing different sports. Always normalize before debating anyone's numbers online.

Women's scale: 16-18 = average trained, 19-20 = athletic, 21-22 = notably muscular, 23+ = elite/national-level. Example: 5'5" woman, 130 lb LBM (59 kg), 1.65 m: 59 / 2.72 = 21.7 — a seriously developed physique. Women's natural ceiling sits ~22-23; claims beyond that without context deserve the same skepticism as men's 26+.

Why LBM Matters

  • Drug dosing: Anesthesia, chemotherapy scale to LBM - fat doesn't metabolize drugs the same way.
  • Protein targets: 1g per lb of lean mass is more precise than per lb of total weight.
  • Metabolic rate: LBM is the primary driver of BMR.
  • Cutting: Track LBM during fat loss - stable LBM + falling weight = pure fat loss (the goal).

Kidney labs: creatinine-based eGFR underestimates kidney function in muscular people — a 220-lb lifter can flag "stage 2 CKD" with perfectly healthy kidneys because creatinine tracks muscle, not just filtration. Cystatin C-based eGFR or a 24-hour urine creatinine clearance resolves it. Tell your doctor you lift before accepting a kidney diagnosis off one lab — the reference ranges were built on average bodies.

Protein math converges, then diverges usefully: 160 lb LBM x 1g = 160g; 200 lb total x 0.8g = 160g — both roads reach the same dinner. They split in obesity: 280 lb at 35% BF = 182 lb LBM → LBM-based target 182g vs weight-based 224g+ — a 40g+ daily difference that decides whether the target is achievable or abandoned by week two. LBM-based dosing is the precision tool; weight-based is the blunt one.

Increasing Lean Mass

  1. Progressive strength training 3-5x/week - the only potent stimulus.
  2. Protein: 0.8-1.0g per lb body weight.
  3. Slight surplus (+200-300 cal) for growth; deficit for cutting.
  4. Sleep 7-9 hours - growth hormone peaks in deep sleep.
  5. Patience: 1-2 lb of muscle/month is excellent for intermediates; beginners may double that.

Newbie gains, quantified: first-year male lifters can add 20-25 lb of muscle (females ~10-12 lb); year two ~10-12; year three ~5-6 — then 1-2 lb/year becomes excellent. Monthly muscle as % of body weight: beginners ~1-1.5%, intermediates ~0.5-1%, advanced ~0.25%. A 180-lb intermediate gaining 1 lb/month of lean mass is winning; scale-plus-mirror confirms it, and impatience is the only thing that can spoil it.

Verify with strength: LBM gains show up as plates — a 1.5x bodyweight squat, 1x bench, and 2x deadlift mark a well-muscled male intermediate. "Maingaining" (eating at maintenance while recomping) works for beginners and detrained lifters; everyone else grows faster in a 200-300 cal surplus. DEXA every 6 months keeps the bulk honest — if LBM stalls 3 months, the program is the problem, not the genetics.
