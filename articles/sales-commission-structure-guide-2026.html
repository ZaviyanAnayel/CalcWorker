Draw mechanics decide who bears ramp risk. A $5K monthly draw against $3K in earned commission leaves a $2K deficit: recoverable means it carries forward against future earnings; non-recoverable means the company absorbs it. New reps should fight for non-recoverable draws during ramp - recoverable draws are just loans against your own future commissions.

Margin-based commission aligns reps with profitable selling. Ten percent of gross margin instead of revenue: $500K in sales at 40% margin = $200K margin base = $20K commission. Discounts now come out of the rep's own check, so the 20%-off-to-close-the-deal habit dies overnight. Pay on what the company keeps, not what the rep books.

Understand draw reconciliation before signing: with a $5,000/month recoverable draw and $3,000 earned commission, you owe the company $2,000 — carried as a deficit. Two slow months creates a $4,000 hole you must dig out of before seeing a commission check again. Non-recoverable draws are safer but rarer. Also clarify clawbacks: if a customer churns within 90 days, many plans reclaim the commission — a rep at 130% attainment can still owe money back in a bad quarter.

OTE Math: $500K Quota Example

OTE = Base Salary + (Quota x Commission Rate at 100%)

Plan: $70K base, $500K quota, 10% commission rate, OTE $120K (70K + 50K).

Tiered accelerators:

  • 0-100% of quota: 10%
  • 100-125%: 15% (accelerator)
  • 125%+: 20% (super-accelerator)

Rep hits 130% ($650K): 50K + (125K x 15%) + (25K x 20%) = 50K + 18.75K + 5K = $73.75K commission. Total: $143.75K.

Run the downside scenario before signing. At 80% of the $500K quota ($400K), the rep earns $400K x 10% = $40K commission plus $70K base = $110K total - 91.7% of the $120K OTE. The base cushions underperformance, which is exactly its purpose. If the downside math does not pay your bills, the OTE was never real.

Decelerators mirror accelerators on the low end. Below 50% quota the rate may drop to 5%: $250K x 5% = $12.5K commission, $82.5K total. Know both ends of the curve before signing - plans advertise the accelerators and bury the decelerators in the fine print.

Typical Rates by Industry

  • SaaS: 8-12% of ARR, OTE $120K-$250K+
  • Real estate: 2.5-3% per side of sale price
  • Insurance: 5-15% first year, 2-5% renewals
  • Retail: 2-5% of sales
  • Recruiting: 20-25% of placed candidate's salary

SDR and AE math differ by role. SDRs typically earn ~30% of OTE on meetings set and qualified pipeline; AEs earn ~10% on closed ARR - a $600K AE quota means $60K variable at plan. Comparing OTE across roles is meaningless; benchmark within the role, and ask what percentage of reps actually hit quota in each.

Car sales invert the SaaS model: around 25% of front-end gross profit per deal - a $2,000-profit car pays $500 - plus volume bonuses kicking in around 12+ units a month. High per-deal rates with brutal quota cliffs, the opposite of SaaS's low rates and smooth accelerators. Every industry's plan reflects how its deals actually work.

Negotiating Your Plan

  1. Uncapped vs capped: Uncapped with accelerators beats higher base with a cap.
  2. Quota realism: 60-70% of reps should hit quota; below 50% means quotas are fantasy.
  3. Clawbacks: Understand when commissions are taken back (churn, cancellations).
  4. Payment timing: Monthly vs quarterly; paid on booking vs cash collection.
  5. Get it in writing - verbal commission promises are worthless.

Fight for quota credit on total contract value. A $300K three-year deal credited as $100K ARR per year pays you one-third of the sale in year one; negotiated TCV credit books the full $300K immediately. Companies prefer ARR accounting for their metrics; reps should prefer TCV credit for their pay - get the crediting rule written into the plan document.

Ramp quotas need their own math. A $500K annual quota ramping 50/75/100/100% by quarter means targets of $62.5K, $93.75K, $125K, $125K - a $406K year-one total, not $500K. A "full" quota with no ramp is a pay cut disguised as a goal; negotiate the ramp schedule, not just the OTE number.
