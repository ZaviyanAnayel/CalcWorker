Estimating Max Heart Rate

Tanaka: Max HR = 208 - 0.7 x Age (better than 220-age)

Age 35: 208 - 24.5 = 183 bpm. Individual variation is 10-15 bpm - field tests (hard 3-minute effort) beat formulas if you want precision.

Karvonen (heart rate reserve): Target = ((Max - Resting) x %) + Resting. With resting 60: 70% zone = ((183-60) x 0.7) + 60 = 146 bpm - more personalized than % of max alone.

Formula face-off at age 50: 220-age = 170, Tanaka 208-35 = 173, Gulati (women) 206-44 = 162 — an 11-bpm spread on zone boundaries, enough to place "Zone 2" anywhere from 97-119 to 102-121. That spread is why formulas are starting points, not answers: a field test (full warm-up, then a 3-minute all-out hill effort, note the peak) beats all three formulas for the price of some suffering.

Resting-HR protocol: seated, morning, before coffee, 3-day average — fit adults sit 40-55, average adults 60-80, and a 5-10 bpm jump over your baseline signals illness, overtraining, or short sleep before you feel it. One caveat: beta-blockers blunt max HR by 20-30 bpm, making every %max formula useless — those users should train by perceived effort and the talk test instead.

Age 35 (max 183): Zone 2 = 110-128 bpm; Zone 4 = 146-165 bpm.

Zone 2 physiology, the reason elites live here: fat oxidation peaks around 60-65% of max, blood lactate stays under 2 mmol/L, and the mitochondrial adaptations from 3-5 weekly hours here exceed what intervals alone produce. Elite endurance athletes spend ~80% of training time in zones 1-2 — the "slow" work builds the engine; intervals just tune it. Skipping base to do intervals is tuning an engine you never built.

The zone 3 trap: 128-146 bpm for our 183-max example feels productively hard but is too intense to recover from daily and too easy to drive top-end adaptation — the gray zone. Runners who live here plateau for years; dropping easy days to true zone 2 (110-128) and pushing one day genuinely hard breaks most plateaus within 8 weeks. Hard days hard, easy days easy — the middle is where progress goes to stall.

The 'Fat Burning Zone' Myth

Zone 2 burns a higher percentage of calories from fat - but fewer total calories. Fat loss math:

  • 30 min Zone 2: ~250 cal, ~60% fat = 150 fat-calories
  • 30 min Zone 4: ~450 cal, ~40% fat = 180 fat-calories

Higher intensity still burns more fat in absolute terms. Zone 2's real value: you can do hours of it weekly without burnout - volume wins for fat loss and aerobic base.

Afterburn math: 30 minutes of zone 4 elevates metabolism 6-15% for hours afterward (EPOC) — roughly 30-70 bonus calories, modest but real. Weekly totals tell the fuller story: 3 x 30 min zone 4 = ~1,350 exercise calories + ~150 EPOC vs 5 x 45 min zone 2 = ~1,875 calories with far less fatigue and injury risk. For pure fat loss, sustainable volume usually beats heroic intensity — you can't out-train a schedule you quit.

The substrate curve, precisely: at 50% max HR ~60% of calories come from fat; at 65% ~50%; at 75% ~35%; at 85% ~15%. The crossover where carbs dominate sits near 60-65% — squarely in zone 2. So the "fat burning zone" is real physiology mislabeled as a shortcut; its true role is building the aerobic base that lets you accumulate the weekly volume where fat loss actually happens.

Zone-Based Week Example

  • Mon: 45 min Zone 2 (easy run/ride)
  • Tue: Intervals: 6x3 min Zone 4, 3 min Zone 1 recovery
  • Wed: Rest or 30 min Zone 1
  • Thu: 40 min Zone 2 + strides
  • Fri: 25 min Zone 3 tempo
  • Sat: 90 min Zone 2 long session
  • Sun: Rest

~80% of time in Zones 1-2, ~20% in Zones 3-5 - the polarized pattern behind most endurance success.

Time-crunched 3-day version: Day 1 — intervals (5 x 3 min zone 4, 3 min easy); Day 2 — 30 min zone 2; Day 3 — 60-75 min zone 2 long. That's ~2.5 hours/week hitting every adaptation that matters. The minimum effective dose for cardiovascular health is 150 min moderate or 75 min vigorous weekly — below that, even 10-minute zone 2 walks count toward the total. Something beats optimal-but-skipped every time.

Progression rules: raise weekly volume max 10%, cut to 60-70% every 4th week (deload), and cap zone 5 work at 1-2 sessions weekly — more spikes injury risk and suppresses immunity. Retest max HR annually; it drifts down ~1 bpm/year, so zones set in 2024 run ~2 bpm high by 2026. Stale zones quietly turn easy days hard.
