Foundations: Credit Scores, Down Payments & Mortgage Insurance

When you start shopping for a mortgage, three numbers dominate the conversation: the minimum credit score required, the down‑payment percentage, and the cost of mortgage insurance. Understanding the math behind each component lets you forecast total out‑of‑pocket costs before you sign a loan estimate.

Monthly Mortgage Insurance (PMI/MIP) = Loan Amount × Annual Rate ÷ 12

For FHA loans the rate is a fixed life‑of‑loan 0.85%‑1.05% of the base loan, while conventional loans use a cancellable PMI that typically starts at 0.30%‑0.55% and drops off once the loan‑to‑value (LTV) falls below 80%.

Credit score thresholds also differ. In 2026 most lenders require:

  • FHA: Minimum 580 for the 3.5% down‑payment option; 500–579 can still qualify with a 10% down payment.
  • Conventional: Minimum 620 for a 3% down payment, though the most competitive rates appear at 700+.

These baselines feed directly into the interest rate you’ll receive, which in turn changes the monthly principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment.

2026 Sample Calculation: $350,000 Purchase

Assume a single‑family home priced at $350,000 in a standard‑limit area. We'll compare a qualified FHA borrower (credit 620) with a conventional borrower (credit 720).

Step 1 – Determine Down Payment

  • FHA 3.5%: $350,000 × 0.035 = $12,250
  • Conventional 3%: $350,000 × 0.03 = $10,500

Step 2 – Calculate Base Loan Amount

  • FHA: $350,000 – $12,250 = $337,750
  • Conventional: $350,000 – $10,500 = $339,500

Step 3 – Apply Interest Rate

2026 average rates: FHA 6.75% APR, Conventional 6.15% APR.

Monthly P&I = L × r × (1+r)^n ÷ [(1+r)^n – 1]
where L = loan amount, r = monthly rate, n = total payments (360 for 30‑yr)
  • FHA monthly rate r = 6.75% ÷ 12 = 0.005625
    Monthly P&I ≈ $2,190
  • Conventional monthly rate r = 6.15% ÷ 12 = 0.005125
    Monthly P&I ≈ $2,074

Step 4 – Add Mortgage Insurance

  • FHA MIP (0.90% annual): $337,750 × 0.009 ÷ 12 = $253 per month (life‑of‑loan)
  • Conventional PMI (0.40% annual): $339,500 × 0.004 ÷ 12 = $113 per month (cancellable after ~7 years)

Step 5 – Estimate Total Monthly Cost

  • FHA: $2,190 + $253 + estimated property tax $350 + HOA $0 = $2,793
  • Conventional: $2,074 + $113 + $350 = $2,537

Even with a slightly larger down payment, the conventional loan saves roughly $256 per month, or $3,072 annually, after the first year. Once the conventional PMI drops off, the gap widens further.

Optimization Strategies to Lower Costs

Whether you choose FHA or conventional, you can shave thousands off your total out‑of‑pocket expense by targeting the variables that matter most.

  1. Boost Your Credit Score. Every 20‑point increase can shave 0.10%‑0.15% off the interest rate, translating to $30‑$45 lower monthly P&I on a $350k loan.
  2. Put More Money Down. Raising the down payment from 3% to 5% reduces the loan balance and LTV, often eliminating PMI for conventional loans entirely.
  3. Shop for the Lowest MIP/PMI Rate. Some FHA lenders offer a discounted upfront MIP (0.6% vs 1.75% standard). For conventional, compare lenders’ PMI tables; a 0.30% rate can be 40% cheaper than 0.55%.
  4. Consider a 15‑Year Fixed. Shorter terms carry lower rates and no PMI for many conventional products, though the monthly payment will be higher; calculate the break‑even point.
  5. Refinance When Eligible. Once your conventional loan reaches 78% LTV, refinance to cancel PMI or switch to a lower‑rate conventional loan to replace FHA MIP.
