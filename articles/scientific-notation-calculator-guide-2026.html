Conversion Rules

N x 10^n, where 1 <= |N| < 10

Large: 93,000,000 = 9.3 x 10^7 (move decimal 7 left).

Small: 0.00000052 = 5.2 x 10^-7 (move decimal 7 right).

Calculator E: 9.3E7 means 9.3 x 10^7 - same thing, compact display.

Small-number conversion is just decimal-hop counting. For 0.00072, move the decimal right until one nonzero digit sits left of it: 7.2, which took 4 hops, so 7.2 x 10^-4. Each hop is one power of ten, and moving right makes the exponent negative. Counting hops beats memorizing zeros - 0.00000052 needs 7 hops, giving 5.2 x 10^-7, with no zero-counting required.

The E on your calculator is scientific notation in disguise: 6.02E23 means 6.02 x 10^23. Use the EE or EXP key to enter it - typing 6.02 x 10 ^ 23 as separate keystrokes evaluates as (6.02 x 10)^23 on many calculators because ^ binds before the implied multiplication. That single keystroke error turns Avogadro's number into nonsense, and it is the most common scientific-notation calculator mistake.

Arithmetic With Powers of 10

  • Multiply: Multiply N's, add exponents. (3 x 10^4)(2 x 10^5) = 6 x 10^9.
  • Divide: Divide N's, subtract exponents. (8 x 10^9)/(2 x 10^3) = 4 x 10^6.
  • Add/subtract: Match exponents first. 3 x 10^5 + 2 x 10^4 = 30 x 10^4 + 2 x 10^4 = 32 x 10^4 = 3.2 x 10^5.
  • Powers: (2 x 10^3)^2 = 4 x 10^6.

Mixed-sign exponents follow the same add-the-exponents rule. (4 x 10^6)(3 x 10^-2): multiply coefficients 4 x 3 = 12, add exponents 6 + (-2) = 4, giving 12 x 10^4, which renormalizes to 1.2 x 10^5. Three distinct steps - coefficients, exponents, renormalize - and the negative exponent changes nothing about the procedure.

Powers of ten turn unit conversion into exponent addition. 1 km = 10^3 m = 10^6 mm, since each step down multiplies by 10^3. Light speed 3 x 10^8 m/s becomes 3 x 10^5 km/s by subtracting 3 from the exponent. Once lengths, masses, and times are all powers of ten, converting is just integer arithmetic on exponents.

Comparing giants means subtracting exponents and ignoring the coefficients. About 10^19 atoms in a grain of sand versus 2 x 10^23 stars in the observable universe: the stars win by a factor of roughly 2 x 10^4, or 20,000 times. When magnitudes differ by orders of ten, the leading digits are decoration - the exponent carries the comparison.

The doubling penny is exponential intuition in one story. Double a penny daily for 30 days: 2^30 is about 1.07 x 10^9 pennies, or $10.7 million. Yet on day 20 the total is only $5,243 - more than 99.9% of the growth lands in the final ten days. Exponents back-load everything, which is why compound interest feels broken until it suddenly does not.

Engineering Notation Cousin

Engineering notation restricts exponents to multiples of 3, matching SI prefixes:

  • 4.7 x 10^3 = 4.7 k (kilo)
  • 2.2 x 10^-6 = 2.2 u (micro)
  • 5 x 10^9 = 5 G (giga)

Electronics lives here: resistors in k-ohms, capacitors in microfarads, frequencies in GHz.

Engineering notation walks down the SI prefix ladder in steps of three. 0.0047 F = 4.7 x 10^-3 F = 4.7 mF = 4,700 uF. Electronics catalogs list capacitors in microfarads and resistors in kilohms, so engineering notation reads component values directly off the datasheet - scientific notation would force a translation step every time.

The multiples-of-three restriction exists because SI prefixes come in threes: 33,000 ohms is 33 k-ohms, 0.0000022 F is 2.2 uF, 2.4 x 10^9 Hz is 2.4 GHz. Scientific notation would write 3.3 x 10^4 ohms, which matches no standard component label. Engineers chose notation that speaks the language of the parts bin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Writing numbers as N x 10^n with 1 <= N < 10. It compactly handles very large and very small numbers.

Move the decimal to make a number between 1 and 10; the moves count becomes the exponent (left = positive, right = negative).

E = 'times 10 to the power of.' 6.02E23 = 6.02 x 10^23.

First match the exponents by adjusting one number, then add the N values and keep the exponent.

Engineering notation uses only exponents divisible by 3 (matching kilo, mega, micro prefixes); scientific uses any exponent with 1 <= N < 10.
