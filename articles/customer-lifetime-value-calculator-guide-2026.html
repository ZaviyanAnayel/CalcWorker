The CLV Formula

CLV = Average Order Value x Purchase Frequency x Customer Lifespan
SaaS CLV = ARPA x Gross Margin % / Churn Rate

Ecommerce: $75 AOV x 4 orders/yr x 3 years = $900 CLV.

SaaS: $200/mo ARPA, 85% margin, 3% monthly churn: 200 x 0.85 / 0.03 = $5,667 CLV.

Margin is the hidden CLV lever the basic formula obscures. The $200/month ARPA example at 85% gross margin yields $5,667 CLV — but at 60% margin (heavy onboarding and support costs) it's $200 × 0.60 ÷ 0.03 = $4,000, a 29% haircut on identical revenue. Ecommerce has the same trap: $75 AOV × 4 orders × 3 years = $900 gross CLV, but at 35% product margin it's only $315 of contribution CLV. Always run the formula on gross-margin dollars, never revenue — revenue-based CLV overstates what you can afford to spend on acquisition by 2-3x, which is how companies scale themselves into losses.

For expansion-revenue businesses, adjust for net revenue retention: effective churn = 1 − NRR. Example: $200 ARPA, 85% margin, 110% NRR — the denominator goes negative, meaning accounts grow faster than they churn and simple-formula CLV is theoretically infinite. In practice, cap the horizon at 5-7 years: $200 × 0.85 × 12 months × 5 years = $10,200 of lifetime contribution per account. Companies with 110%+ NRR can profitably sustain LTV:CAC ratios that look reckless on the simple formula — the expansion revenue is doing the heavy lifting.

The LTV:CAC Ratio

LTV:CAC = CLV / Customer Acquisition Cost (target 3:1+)
RatioMeaning
Under 1:1Losing money on every customer
1:1 - 3:1Surviving, not thriving
3:1 - 5:1Healthy, scalable
Over 5:1Under-investing in growth

Example: $900 CLV, $250 CAC = 3.6:1 - healthy. You can afford to spend up to $300 acquiring customers.

A blended 3.6:1 ratio hides channel truth — split it before scaling. Paid search might run $900 CLV ÷ $425 CAC = 2.1:1 while content/SEO runs $1,100 CLV ÷ $140 CAC = 7.9:1. The number that matters is the marginal ratio: what your next dollar of spend acquires. If the next $50K of paid spend acquires at 1.8:1, you're scaling losses while the blended number smiles at you. Audit quarterly: kill or fix any channel under 2:1 within 90 days, hold 2:1-3:1 steady, and double down on anything above 4:1.

A 4:1 ratio with an 18-month payback is a cash trap wearing a healthy mask. Compare: $250 CAC on $75/month contribution pays back in 3.3 months — fine. But enterprise deals at $15K CAC on $50K CLV (3.3:1) with 24-month payback burn $15K of cash per customer for two years before breaking even — grow fast and you bankrupt yourself with "profitable" customers. Pair the ratio with a payback ceiling — 12 months for SMB, 18 for enterprise — and reject deals that pass the ratio but breach the payback limit.

Increasing CLV

  1. Reduce churn: 3% to 2% monthly churn nearly doubles SaaS CLV (5,667 to 8,500).
  2. Upsell/cross-sell: Raise ARPA 20%: CLV rises 20%.
  3. Improve margins: Self-serve onboarding cuts service costs.
  4. Loyalty programs: Lift purchase frequency - 4 to 5 orders/yr = +25% CLV.
  5. Win-back campaigns: Reactivating churned customers is 5x cheaper than new acquisition.

Annual prepay is the highest-ROI churn lever available. Moving a $29/month customer to $290/year (a 17% discount, "two months free") typically cuts churn 40-60% because the renewal decision happens once instead of twelve times. On the $200 ARPA example, dropping monthly churn from 3% to 1.5% via annual plans lifts CLV from $5,667 to $11,333 — doubling lifetime value while pulling cash forward. Frame it as bonus time rather than a discount: "2 months free" converts better than "17% off" at identical economics.

Expansion revenue compounds CLV without acquisition cost: land 10 seats at $200/month ($2K MRR), expand to 25 seats over 18 months ($5K MRR), and the account's lifetime value roughly triples versus a flat account. Productize the expansion path — usage-based tiers, $49-$99/month add-on modules, and annual business reviews that surface unused capacity. Win-back math is equally sharp: a $5 email/SMS sequence reactivating churned customers at 8% conversion costs $62.50 per reactivation on a $120/month product, versus $250+ to acquire a new customer.

Cohort Analysis

Average CLV hides the truth. Segment by:

  • Acquisition channel: Organic customers often have 2x the CLV of paid ones.
  • Cohort month: Are newer cohorts retaining better?
  • Plan tier: Enterprise vs SMB economics differ wildly.

Payback period matters too: CAC $250, $75/mo contribution: payback = 3.3 months. Under 12 months is healthy for SMB SaaS.

Read retention as a curve, not a single number. A healthy SaaS cohort retains roughly 92% at month 1, 78% at month 3, 65% at month 6, and 55% at month 12. If your June cohort sits at 60% in month 3 while March held 80%, something changed — pricing, onboarding, or a bad release — and the CLV gap is measurable: at $200 ARPA and 85% margin, the difference between 2% and 3.5% monthly churn is $8,500 versus $4,857 CLV. Cohort curves surface the leak quarters before the blended churn number moves, while there's still time to fix it.

Segment CLV by channel to reallocate spend with confidence: organic cohorts at $1,050 CLV with $120 CAC (8.75:1) versus paid social at $640 CLV with $310 CAC (2.06:1). Shifting $100K/quarter from paid social to content trades ~322 low-quality customers for ~833 high-quality ones — total CLV acquired jumps from $206K to $875K on the same budget. Track payback per cohort too: a cohort paying back in 5 months funds its own replacement, while a 14-month-payback cohort needs external financing to scale.
