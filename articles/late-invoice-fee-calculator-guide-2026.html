Standard Late Fee Rates

Late Fee = Overdue Amount x Monthly Rate x Months Late

Common: 1.5%/month (18% APR). $5,000 invoice, 60 days late: 5,000 x 0.015 x 2 = $150.

RateAPR EquivalentTypical Use
1%/month12%Conservative B2B
1.5%/month18%Standard
2%/month24%Aggressive/retail

Simple versus compounding interest changes the total - disclose your method. At 1.5% monthly for 6 months, simple interest gives 9%; compounding gives 1.015^6 - 1 = 9.34%. The gap is small but the ambiguity favors the debtor in disputes, so state "compounded monthly" or "simple interest" explicitly in the contract clause.

Flat fees hit small invoices hardest. A $50 flat fee plus 1.5%/month on a $2,000 invoice 90 days late totals $50 + $90 = $140 - a 7% effective charge. On a $500 invoice the same structure is 28%. Scale the structure to invoice size: percentage-only for small bills, flat-plus-percentage once invoices clear a few thousand.

Legal Ground Rules

  • Must be in the contract - you generally can't add fees retroactively.
  • State usury caps limit rates (often 10-18% for non-loans; varies widely).
  • Reasonable and disclosed: Courts enforce agreed fees; surprise penalties get tossed.
  • Government clients: Often exempt or have statutory interest (federal: Prompt Payment Act).
  • Include the fee clause on the invoice itself, not just the contract.

State caps vary enough to matter. California defaults to 10% annually without a written agreement; Texas allows 18% on written contracts. A "standard 1.5% per month" clause that is enforceable in one state may be cut down in another. If you bill clients across state lines, have the clause reviewed for each state where your biggest debtors sit.

The invoice itself can serve as the contract. One printed line - "Balances unpaid after 30 days accrue 1.5% per month" - makes fees enforceable even with no master services agreement, provided the client accepted the terms (a reply saying "agreed" counts). Put the clause on every invoice, not just the contract, so it is in front of the client at every billing.

Invoice Terms That Get You Paid

  1. Net 15 beats Net 30 - shorter terms get paid faster with no downside.
  2. 2/10 Net 30: 2% discount if paid in 10 days - effective 36% APR incentive to pay early.
  3. Upfront deposits: 30-50% before work starts filters serious clients.
  4. Milestone billing: Never let receivables exceed 25% of project value.
  5. Auto-reminders: Day 1, 7, 14, 30 - polite automation beats awkward calls.

The 2/10 Net 30 discount is wildly generous in APR terms: 2% for paying 20 days early annualizes to roughly 36% (2% x 365/20). Any client who can borrow below 36% should take the discount every time - which is why CFOs love it and why offering it accelerates cash flow so effectively. Price the discount as a financing cost and it is still cheaper than most credit lines.

Send-day affects payment speed for free. Invoices emailed Tuesday through Thursday mornings get paid fastest; Friday invoices sit untouched until Monday and slip about three days on average. Automate your billing to hit mid-week mornings - billing rhythm is cash flow you do not have to negotiate for.

Escalation Ladder

  • Day 1-7: Friendly automated reminder (often just forgotten).
  • Day 14: Personal email + late fee notice.
  • Day 30: Phone call, pause current work.
  • Day 45: Formal demand letter citing contract terms.
  • Day 60+: Collections agency (25-40% contingency) or small claims court (up to $5K-$10K by state).

A demand letter needs five elements: the amount owed, invoice numbers and dates, the exact contract clause authorizing fees, a firm deadline (10 days is standard), and the stated next step if unpaid. Vague threats get ignored; specific letters citing clause numbers and calendar dates get paid. Send it certified or with read receipt - proof of delivery matters if this reaches court.

Collections versus court is a math problem. On a $4,200 debt, an agency at 30% contingency costs $1,260 but keeps your hands clean; small claims court costs about $75 in filing fees and keeps 100% but costs you a day. Under roughly $2,000 most agencies will not take the case, so court wins by default - file, show the contract and the invoice, collect.
