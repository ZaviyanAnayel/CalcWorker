Trip Cost Formula

Trip Cost = (Distance / MPG) x Price per Gallon

500-mile trip, 30 MPG, $3.50/gal: (500/30) x 3.50 = 16.67 x 3.50 = $58.33.

Cost per mile: 3.50/30 = $0.117/mile - the number that makes vehicle comparisons instant.

Multi-leg trips compound the math fast. A 1,200-mile road trip at 28 MPG and $3.40/gal: (1,200 ÷ 28) × 3.40 = $145.71 in fuel. Add a 150-mile detour and it's $163.93 — the detour's true cost is $18.21, which reframes every "should we stop there?" debate. Split four ways, the base trip is $36.43 per person — versus $145.71 × 4 = $582.84 if everyone drives separately. Carpool math is the strongest fuel saver in existence: per-person cost falls 50-75% with 2-4 riders, beating every hypermiling trick combined.

Annualize it for budgeting: 12,000 miles/year at 30 MPG and $3.50/gal = $1,400/year ($117/month). Move to a 40-MPG hybrid and it's $1,050/year — $350/year saved, $1,750 over five years. The cost-per-mile figure ($0.117) also prices alternatives instantly: an $89 flight versus $58.33 in fuel for the 500-mile trip — the flight wins on cash until you add $30 airport parking and 3 hours of airport time, at which point the car's effective economics change completely.

Track actual MPG: miles driven / gallons pumped over 3+ fill-ups. Most cars deliver 10-15% below EPA ratings in real driving.

The EPA gap varies by powertrain. Conventional pickups and SUVs miss EPA combined ratings by 12-18% in real driving, sedans by 8-12%, while hybrids land within ~5% (their city-cycle advantage survives traffic). Winter gasoline blends cost another 2-5% (butane-heavy winter fuel carries less energy), and E10 ethanol's lower energy density shaves ~3% versus ethanol-free gas — a "cheaper" E10 fill at $0.10/gal less can be net-negative on cost per mile. Track consumption with the 3-fill-up method: one tank lies, three tell the truth.

Speed is the dominant variable most drivers ignore. Fuel economy peaks around 50-55 mph; each 5 mph above 50 costs roughly 7-8% — 75 mph versus 65 mph burns ~15% more, turning 30 MPG into 25.5 and the $58.33 trip into $68.63. Cruise control on flat highways saves 5-7% by eliminating throttle oscillation (in hills it can cost 2-3% versus a skilled foot). Roof boxes are the silent killer: a 10-25% highway penalty — move cargo inside or to a rear hitch carrier (3-5% penalty) instead.

EV vs Gas Breakeven

Gas car: $0.117/mile fuel. EV: 0.30 kWh/mile x $0.15/kWh = $0.045/mile.

Savings: $0.072/mile. At 12,000 miles/yr: $864/yr.

Breakeven on $8,000 EV premium: 8,000/864 = 9.3 years on fuel alone. Add lower maintenance (~$400/yr) and tax credits, and breakeven often falls to 4-6 years.

2026 charging economics split three ways. Home charging at $0.15/kWh = $0.045/mile. Public DC fast charging at $0.45-$0.55/kWh = $0.135-$0.165/mile — triple the cost, roughly at parity with a 30-MPG gas car at $3.50/gal ($0.117/mile). An EV road-tripping exclusively on fast chargers barely beats gas on fuel. The EV advantage lives at home: 12,000 miles at $0.045 = $540/year versus $1,400 for gas — that $860/year savings needs ~9 years to cover an $8,000 price premium on fuel alone.

The breakeven math improves with the full picture. EVs save ~$400-$600/year on maintenance (no oil changes, fewer brake jobs via regen, no transmission service), and 2026 federal/state incentives can cut $3,750-$7,500 off the purchase price — an $8,000 premium net of a $5,000 credit is really $3,000, breakeven in ~2.5 years ($3,000 ÷ ~$1,260 combined annual savings). Plug-in hybrids split the difference: 30-40 miles of electric range covers ~70% of daily trips electrically while gas handles road trips — often the lowest 5-year cost of all three options.

Cutting Fuel Costs

  1. Slow down: 75 mph vs 65 mph burns ~15% more fuel.
  2. Combine trips: Cold starts are the thirstiest miles.
  3. Tire pressure: Check monthly - free 3% savings.
  4. Remove weight: Every 100 lb costs ~1% MPG.
  5. Gas apps: Price differences of $0.30+/gal are common on the same street.
  6. Cruise control on highways smooths throttle input.

Price dispersion is free money. The same street routinely shows $0.30-$0.50/gal spreads — on a 15-gallon fill that's $4.50-$7.50 per stop, $230-$390/year for weekly fillers. Gas-price apps surface the spread in seconds; the catch is detour cost — driving 10 minutes out of the way for $0.10/gal saves $1.50 while burning ~$1.00 of fuel and 20 minutes. Rule: only chase spreads above $0.20/gal within your existing route. Cash discounts ($0.05-$0.10/gal at independents) stack on top of the spread.

Warehouse clubs sell gas $0.20-$0.35/gal below street price: at $0.25 savings × 15 gallons × 50 fill-ups = $187.50/year — triple a $60 membership on fuel alone. Tire pressure remains the cheapest efficiency mod: 1 PSI under across all tires costs ~0.3% MPG, and the average car runs 5-8 PSI low — a monthly 2-minute check recovers 2-3% ($30-$45/year). Wheel alignment matters too: misaligned wheels drag like a constant headwind, costing 2-5% until a $80-$150 alignment service fixes it — payback in months.
