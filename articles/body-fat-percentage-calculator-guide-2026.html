The Navy Method: Tape Measure Formula

Men: 86.010 x log10(waist-neck) - 70.041 x log10(height) + 36.76
Women: 163.205 x log10(waist+hip-neck) - 97.684 x log10(height) - 78.387

(inches). Example man: waist 34", neck 16", height 70": 86.010 x log10(18) - 70.041 x log10(70) + 36.76 = about 17.5%. Accurate within 3-4% for most - free and repeatable.

Work the women's formula: waist 30", hip 38", neck 13", height 65". Compute 163.205 x log10(30+38-13 = 55) = 163.205 x 1.7404 = 284.0; minus 97.684 x log10(65) = 97.684 x 1.8129 = 177.1; minus 78.387 → 28.5%. Sensitivity check: a 1" waist mismeasure shifts the result ~2 percentage points — the tape measure, not the logarithm, is the weak link in this method.

Standardize or don't bother: post-meal bloating adds 0.5-1" to the waist; measuring flexed vs relaxed changes the neck reading; a heavy training day's pump skews everything. The protocol is morning, fasted, post-bathroom, same tape position, relaxed exhale. Under those conditions the Navy method's 3-4% error band is honest; under random conditions it's fiction.

Women need higher essential fat for hormonal health - sub-15% in women often disrupts cycles. Men below ~6% face testosterone and immune suppression.

Age reshapes the averages: American men 50-59 average ~28-30% body fat, women ~38-40% — "average" is not "healthy." Post-menopause, estrogen loss redistributes fat viscerally: a 2-4" waist gain with zero scale change is typical. The counterproof: competitive master athletes over 60 hold 15-20% (men) and 22-28% (women) — the age curve is mostly lifestyle wearing an age costume.

Sport-specific reference points: male marathoners 6-10%, soccer players 10-14%, NFL linemen 25-30% (functional mass at 300+ lb). Female gymnasts cluster 14-18%. Combat sports deserve a warning: cutting below ~8% (men) risks hormonal crash, which is why wrestling programs now cap losses at 1.5% of body weight per week with hydration testing — the scale weight must be real, not dehydrated.

Method Accuracy Ranked

  1. DEXA scan: 1-2% error, gold standard; $50-$150 per scan.
  2. Hydrostatic weighing: ~2%, inconvenient.
  3. Bod Pod: 2-3%.
  4. Skinfold calipers: 3-4% (skilled tester).
  5. Navy tape method: 3-4%, free.
  6. Smart scales (BIA): 5-8% - hydration swings make single readings unreliable, but trends work.

Pick one method and stick with it. Consistency beats accuracy for tracking change.

BIA smart-scale traps, quantified: evening readings run 2-5 points higher than morning (food, water, skin temperature); callused feet block the electrical current and inflate readings; "athlete mode" just swaps in a different equation — it doesn't measure anything better. DEXA's fine print: ±1-2% from hydration swings, $50-150 per scan, and a radiation dose (~1-5 microsieverts) smaller than a cross-country flight — safe, but not something to repeat monthly.

The practical stack: monthly Navy tape (free, tracks change) + annual or semiannual DEXA (anchors truth) + weekly scale weight (catches drift). Skip the $30 scale upgrade if you already own calipers and a tape — three consistent cheap methods beat one expensive inconsistent one. And never compare readings across methods; a 17% Navy and a 21% BIA on the same body is normal disagreement, not a crisis.

Tracking Fat Loss Correctly

  • Measure same time of day, same conditions (morning, fasted).
  • Track fat mass and lean mass separately: losing 10 lb with 8 lb fat + 2 lb muscle beats losing 12 lb with 6 lb muscle.
  • Strength train during cuts - it preserves muscle, keeping fat loss "pure."
  • Expect 0.5-1% body fat loss per month at a sustainable pace.

Recomposition math, the motivating kind: 180 lb at 25% body fat = 45 lb fat + 135 lb lean. Six months of lifting at maintenance → 180 lb at 20% = 36 lb fat + 144 lb lean: 9 lb of fat swapped for 9 lb of muscle with the scale frozen. The mirror and tape catch what the scale misses — waist down 2", arms up 0.5", same body weight, visibly different person.

Stall protocol: no waist or body-fat change for 3 straight weeks → cut 150-200 cal/day or add 2,000 daily steps, not both. Take photos monthly — front, side, back, same lighting, same time of day — because memory edits your body image weekly. Keep protein at 1g/lb through every cut; muscle retained is what makes the lost weight pure fat.
