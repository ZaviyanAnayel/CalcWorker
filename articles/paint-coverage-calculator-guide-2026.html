The Gallon Formula

Gallons = (Wall Area - Openings) / Coverage per Gallon x Coats

12x15 ft room, 8 ft ceilings:

  • Wall area: 2(12x8) + 2(15x8) = 192 + 240 = 432 sq ft
  • Minus door (21) + 2 windows (30): 432 - 51 = 381 sq ft
  • Coverage 350 sq ft/gal, 2 coats: (381/350) x 2 = 2.2 gallons - buy 3

Metric version: liters = (wall m² − openings) ÷ 10 m² per liter × coats. A 4×5 m room with 2.5 m ceilings: walls = 2(4×2.5) + 2(5×2.5) = 45 m², minus door (2) and window (3) = 40 m²; at 10 m²/L × 2 coats = 8 liters — buy 10 (two 5L cans). Sprayers change the math: add 15-20% waste for overspray and purging — the 2.2-gallon example becomes ~2.6 gallons, so you'd still buy 3, but a 1.1-gallon calc becomes 1.3 and crosses the buy-2 threshold.

The rounding rule is always up, never down. Paint is $30-$60/gallon, but a mid-wall run-out costs a visible lap mark or a second store trip ($10 of gas plus an hour). For the 12×15 example at 2.2 gallons, buy 3 — the leftover quart covers touch-ups for years. Pros buy 10% extra as policy; on a 10-gallon exterior job that's one extra $45 gallon insuring a $4,000 paint job. And measure twice: recheck ceiling height (9-ft ceilings add 12.5% wall area over 8-ft) before finalizing the order.

Brand tier moves the numbers honestly. Premium paints ($45-$70/gal) genuinely cover 350-400 sq ft on second coats with better hide; budget paints ($25-$35/gal) often manage only 250-300 and need the extra coat the label doesn't mention — making the "cheap" gallon the expensive one. Same job: 3 budget gallons at $30 = $90 versus 2 premium at $50 = $100, plus your extra labor for the third coat. Sheen matters too: flat hides best (fewer coats over repairs), while gloss/semigloss shows every flaw and can demand an extra pass for uniformity.

Rough surfaces drink paint: brick and concrete block cover ~150-200 sq ft/gal, stucco ~200-250, rough-sawn wood ~200. A 1,500 sq ft brick exterior at 175 sq ft/gal needs 8.6 gallons per coat — 18 gallons for two coats, not the 9 a smooth-wall calc suggests. Dark-over-light color changes add a full coat regardless of surface: deep red over white primer still wants 3 topcoats. When in doubt, buy the extra gallon — unopened paint returns to the store; a starved second coat doesn't.

Primer Rules

  • Always prime: New drywall, bare wood, dramatic color changes, stains.
  • Skip primer: Same-color repaints on sealed walls in good condition.
  • Tinted primer: Gray under dark colors cuts topcoats from 3 to 2.
  • Primer + paint combos: Fine for repaints, not for bare surfaces.

New-drywall math makes primer non-negotiable. Unprimed drywall and joint compound absorb ~50% more topcoat — the 12×15 room would drink 3+ gallons of $45 paint ($135+) instead of 1 gallon of $18 PVA primer + 2 gallons of paint ($108). The primer literally pays you $27 to use it, plus better adhesion. Tinted primer's value is precise: gray primer under deep colors (navy, charcoal, red) cuts topcoats from 3 to 2 — saving a full $45 gallon and ~3 hours of labor on the example room.

Specialty primers earn their $25-$35/gallon. Shellac-based for water stains, smoke damage, and tannin bleed — the only thing that stops knot bleed-through on pine (latex primers fail within months). Bonding primer for glossy oil-painted trim and tile, with 2-3x the adhesion of latex-on-gloss. Masonry primer with efflorescence resistance for basement walls. Using "paint + primer" combo products on these problems is the #1 DIY primer mistake — they're thickened topcoats, not real primers, and stains bleed through within a year.

Cost Breakdown and Pro Tips

12x15 room, DIY: 3 gal paint ($45/gal) = $135 + primer $30 + supplies $40 = ~$205. Pro job: $400-$800.

  1. Buy 10% extra - running out mid-wall guarantees visible lap marks.
  2. Box your paint: Mix all gallons together for color consistency.
  3. Cut in first, then roll - maintain a wet edge.
  4. Two thin coats beat one thick coat (adhesion, durability).
  5. Save the label - color codes for touch-ups later.

Professional pricing runs $2-$6 per sq ft of floor area ($400-$800 for the 12×15 room = 180 sq ft × ~$3-4), or $25-$75/hour plus materials — the spread reflects prep work, which is 60-70% of a quality job (patching, sanding, caulking, masking). A $400 quote that skips prep versus a $750 quote with full prep: the cheap job fails in 2-3 years, the good one lasts 8-10. Get itemized quotes; "2 coats premium paint" with no prep line items is a red flag, not a bargain.

Paint tier economics over a decade: budget ($28/gal, repaints every ~4 years) versus premium ($55/gal, every 8-10 years with better washability). Two budget repaints of the example room: 6 gallons × $28 = $168 + two weekends of labor. One premium job: 3 gallons × $55 = $165 + one weekend. Same paint cost, half the labor, better finish — premium wins unless you're flipping the property. Store leftovers sealed, upside-down (skin forms on the bottom instead), at 50-80°F: good paint lasts 5+ years for touch-ups.
