The Two Exclusions

Exclusion2026 AmountHow It Works
Annual exclusion$19,000 per recipientTax-free, no return needed; spouses can split to $38,000
Lifetime exemption$15M per personUnified with estate tax; excess gifts use it up
Taxable Gift = Gift Amount − $19,000 (annual exclusion per recipient)

Give $50,000 to your child: $31,000 is a taxable gift — reported on Form 709, but no tax owed until you exhaust the $15M lifetime exemption.

529 superfunding mechanics for 2026: contribute $95,000 single ($190,000 as a couple splitting gifts) at birth and elect the 5-year spread on Form 709 — it counts as $19,000/year per beneficiary against the annual exclusion. At 7% growth for 18 years, that single $95,000 becomes roughly $321,000 for college. You must file Form 709 to make the election even though no tax is due — skip the form and the whole contribution counts against year one.

Direct payments are unlimited: $60,000/year in tuition paid straight to the university plus $40,000 in medical bills paid straight to the hospital = $100,000/year moved with no return filed and no exemption touched. The routing is everything — write the check to the institution, never to the child. Money that passes through the kid's hands first becomes a taxable gift; money that never touches them doesn't.

Gift Tax Rates

Taxable gifts beyond the lifetime exemption face 18-40% marginal rates (top 40% above $1.06M). In practice, fewer than 0.1% of estates ever pay — the exemption covers nearly everyone.

2026 law: The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025) made the higher exemption permanent at $15M per person ($30M per couple), indexed for inflation from 2027. The once-feared sunset to ~$7M is gone — plan gifts for strategy, not deadlines.

For 2026 the lifetime exemption stands at $15 million per person ($30 million per couple) and is now permanent law — the One Big Beautiful Bill Act removed the once-scheduled sunset entirely, with inflation indexing from 2027. The frantic pre-2026 gifting rush is over; plan gifts for strategy now (valuation discounts, shifting future growth out of the estate), not panic. If your estate is under $15 million, the exemption is effectively a non-issue for the foreseeable future.

Above the exemption, valuation discounts do the heavy lifting. Transfer $1 million of family LLC interests appraised with 30% combined discounts (lack of marketability + minority interest) = a $700,000 gift for tax purposes — shifting $1 million of value while consuming only $700,000 of exemption, saving 40% × $300,000 = $120,000 in future tax. This requires a qualified appraisal; the IRS routinely challenges aggressive discounts, so the $5,000-$15,000 appraisal fee is mandatory armor, not optional.

Tax-Free Giving Strategies

  1. Annual exclusion gifts: A couple with 3 kids + spouses = 6 recipients × $38,000 = $228,000/year tax-free, no lifetime impact.
  2. Direct payments: Tuition and medical bills paid directly to the institution are unlimited and exclusion-free.
  3. 529 superfunding: Front-load 5 years of annual exclusions ($95,000 single / $190,000 couple) into a 529 at once.
  4. Spousal gifts: Unlimited to citizen spouses.

Crummey trusts multiply the annual exclusion: $19,000 × 3 grandchildren = $57,000/year into an irrevocable trust, each gift qualifying through 30-day withdrawal rights (Crummey powers) given to the beneficiaries. The unglamorous part is the compliance: you must send written Crummey notices every year. No notice, no exclusion — and the IRS has won cases on this technicality alone. Calendar the notices alongside the contributions.

Fund Roth IRAs for working kids: a teen earning $7,500 can receive a $7,500 custodial Roth contribution (2026 limit) — grandparents may gift the cash, since the contribution just can't exceed the child's earned income. At 8% for 50 years, that's $351,700 of tax-free wealth from one summer job. It's the highest-ROI gift in the tax code, and it teaches the habit that built it.

Gifts vs Inheritance: Basis Matters

Critical difference: Gifted assets carry your cost basis; inherited assets get a step-up to market value.

Gift $100K of stock (bought at $20K): recipient owes tax on $80K gain when selling. Die holding it: heirs' basis steps to $100K — $0 tax. For highly appreciated assets, holding until death usually beats lifetime gifting — unless exemption sunset or estate size forces action.

Run the $107,000 decision before gifting appreciated stock. You hold $500,000 of stock with a $50,000 basis. Gift it: the heir inherits your basis and pays 23.8% × $450,000 = $107,100 when selling. Hold until death: the step-up wipes the gain to $0. Lifetime gifting of highly appreciated assets destroys six figures of value — the right move unless estate tax (40% above $15 million) is the bigger threat. For most families, hold beats gift on appreciated assets.

Community-property states double the bonus: at the first spouse's death, both halves of community property step up — $1 million of joint stock with a $200,000 basis becomes a $1 million basis overnight. In common-law states, only the decedent's half steps up, leaving $400,000 of embedded gain. Where you live changes the math by six figures; relocating in retirement years has tax consequences nobody mentions at the open house.
