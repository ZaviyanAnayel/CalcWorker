Rhombus and kite areas skip base-times-height entirely: area = (diagonal1 x diagonal2)/2. A kite with diagonals 8 and 10 covers (8 x 10)/2 = 40 square units. This formula also explains the triangle's 1/2: a triangle is exactly half of its bounding parallelogram, so the half is structural, not arbitrary. Whenever a shape can be framed as half of something simpler, the 1/2 appears.

Unit conversion is where area arithmetic silently breaks. A 12-inch by 12-inch tile is 144 square inches, and 1 square foot = 144 square inches - not 12. Carpet at $3 per square foot for a 10 x 12 foot room: 120 sq ft x $3 = $360. Convert units before multiplying, never after: mixing inches and feet in one formula produces answers off by a factor of 144.

Circle Deep Dive

Area = pi x r^2 | Circumference = 2 x pi x r

Example: Circular garden, diameter 20 ft (r = 10): Area = 3.1416 x 100 = 314.2 sq ft. Sod at $0.50/sq ft = $157.

Sector: Slice with angle theta: Area = (theta/360) x pi x r^2. A 90-degree slice of r=10: 0.25 x 314.2 = 78.5 sq ft.

Ring-shaped regions use the annulus formula: outer area minus inner area. A washer with outer radius 10 mm and hole radius 7 mm has area pi x (100 - 49) = 51pi, about 160.2 sq mm. Running tracks, pipe cross-sections, and garden beds around circular fountains all reduce to this subtraction. Any time material forms a band around an empty center, think annulus.

Arc length answers a different question than sector area: how much edging, not how much sod. A 120-degree arc of radius 6 has length (120/360) x 2 x pi x 6 = 4pi, about 12.57 units. Fencing a curved flower bed needs arc length; mulching it needs sector area. Match the formula to the physical thing being bought - edging is sold by the foot, mulch by the square foot.

Composite Shapes

L-shaped room: Split into rectangles. 20x15 main + 8x10 extension: 300 + 80 = 380 sq ft.

Room with circular rug cutout: Floor 12x12 = 144; rug r=4: 50.3; exposed floor = 93.7 sq ft.

Strategy: decompose into known shapes, add or subtract. Sketch and label every dimension first.

Semicircles graft onto rectangles in stages, ramps, and floor plans. A 10 x 6 stage with a semicircular end of radius 5: rectangle 60 plus half-circle (pi x 25)/2 = 39.27, totaling 99.27 square units. Add the parts; never hunt for one mega-formula. Label each sub-shape's dimensions on the sketch before computing anything - most composite-shape errors are labeling errors.

"Shaded area" contest problems almost always mean big-minus-small. A 10 x 10 square with an inscribed circle of radius 5 cut out leaves 100 - 78.54 = 21.46 of shaded region. The strategy generalizes: area between a path and a lawn, paint around a window, the border of a photo mat. Identify the whole, subtract the hole.

Practical Applications

  • Paint: Wall area minus doors/windows (door ~21 sq ft, window ~15 sq ft). One gallon covers ~350 sq ft.
  • Flooring: Add 10% waste factor for cuts.
  • Lawn: Subtract house footprint, driveway, beds from lot area.
  • Garden beds: Area x depth = soil volume (divide cubic feet by 27 for cubic yards).

Wallpaper math punishes anyone who forgets to round up. A 12 x 8 foot wall is 96 sq ft; minus a 15 sq ft window leaves 81. If a roll covers 56 sq ft, 81/56 = 1.45 rolls - and you must buy 2, since partial rolls are not sold and pattern matching wastes extra. Materials always round up; the "waste factor" is not pessimism, it is how the store sells.

Concrete extends area into volume with one division: a 12 x 10 foot patio, 4 inches thick: 120 x (4/12) = 40 cubic feet, divided by 27 = 1.48 cubic yards - order 2. Concrete sells by the cubic yard, so area x depth / 27 is the entire ordering formula. Thickness in feet is the step everyone forgets: 4 inches is 4/12 feet, not 4.
