Calculating BMR

Men: 10 x weight(kg) + 6.25 x height(cm) - 5 x age + 5
Women: 10 x weight(kg) + 6.25 x height(cm) - 5 x age - 161

Example woman: 35 yrs, 150 lb (68 kg), 5'5" (165 cm): 680 + 1031 - 175 - 161 = 1,375 cal/day.

BMR is typically 60-70% of total daily burn - the foundation everything else builds on.

Male example: 45 years, 200 lb (90.7 kg), 6'0" (183 cm): 907 + 1,144 - 225 + 5 = 1,831 cal/day. Cross-check with the older Harris-Benedict: ~1,900 for the same man — the ~70-cal gap is typical, and Mifflin-St Jeor is preferred because it was validated against modern indirect calorimetry rather than 1919 lab data. Use one formula consistently; switching formulas mid-diet fabricates phantom progress out of arithmetic.

Lab truth: indirect calorimetry (breathing into a metabolic cart for 15-20 min, $75-150 at sports labs) measures actual resting burn within ~3%. Wearable "resting calorie" figures are estimates dressed as measurements — treat them as ±15%. And true BMR requires overnight fast plus complete rest; what most people (and apps) call BMR is really RMR, ~10% higher — another quiet 150-cal discrepancy.

What Raises and Lowers BMR

FactorEffect on BMR
More muscle massUp (~6 cal/lb muscle/day)
Aging (per decade)Down 1-2%
Prolonged dietingDown 5-15% (adaptive thermogenesis)
HyperthyroidismUp significantly
Cold exposureUp temporarily
High protein dietUp slightly (thermic effect)

Muscle's effect is real but modest - 10 lb of new muscle adds ~60 cal/day, not the 500 some claim. Its bigger value is enabling harder training.

Thyroid numbers: subclinical hypothyroidism (TSH 4.5-10) can suppress BMR 100-200 cal/day; treated patients regain it — "my thyroid" is sometimes a real 150-cal explanation, not an excuse. Fever raises metabolic rate ~13% per 1°C of body temperature: a 102°F flu burns ~250 extra calories daily, which is why appetite vanishes and weight drops during illness. Neither is a weight-loss strategy; both prove BMR is hormonal, not fixed.

Life-stage surcharges: second-trimester pregnancy +~300 cal/day, breastfeeding +~500 (milk production is metabolically expensive), growing teenagers +10-15% over adult formulas. Quitting smoking drops BMR ~200 cal/day as nicotine's stimulant effect exits — the famous post-quit weight gain is ~60% metabolic and ~40% hand-to-mouth snacking, entirely manageable with a deliberate 200-cal adjustment instead of surprise.

BMR vs TDEE

TDEE = BMR x Activity Multiplier (1.2 - 1.9)

BMR is the idle engine; TDEE adds movement, exercise, and digestion (thermic effect of food ~10%). Never set calorie targets from BMR alone - that is starvation-level for active people. Use BMR as the floor: sustained eating below BMR risks muscle loss, hormonal disruption, and rebound.

Full stack for our 1,375-BMR woman at 1.55 activity: TDEE = 2,131. Inside that multiplier hides the thermic effect of food (~10%, ~210 cal) and NEAT — which ranges 300-800 cal/day between a fidgety walker and a still sitter at the same "activity level." Two people, same BMR, same workouts, 500-cal different TDEEs: NEAT is usually the entire explanation, and it's why "we eat the same but she stays thin" is almost always a steps story.

The floor rule, quantified: sustained eating below BMR (1,375 for her) for weeks triggers adaptive thermogenesis — TDEE drops 5-15% as the body economizes, so a 1,200-cal diet soon maintains instead of losing. The fix isn't lower calories but diet breaks: 10-14 days at maintenance restores most of the adaptation. Chronic 1,200-cal dieting is how people end up maintaining on 1,400 — the metabolism didn't break, it adapted exactly as designed.

Boosting Your Metabolism Honestly

  1. Strength train - the only lasting way to raise BMR (via muscle).
  2. Eat enough protein - maximizes thermic effect.
  3. Move more daily - NEAT (steps) affects TDEE more than BMR, but matters more overall.
  4. Avoid crash diets - they suppress BMR for months.
  5. Sleep - deprivation measurably drops metabolic rate.

Cold thermogenesis, honestly: 2 hours at 60°F in light clothing burns ~100-200 extra calories (shivering plus brown-fat activation); a 5-minute cold shower burns maybe 20-50 — negligible. Ice baths post-lifting are actively counterproductive: they blunt the inflammatory signal that drives muscle growth. Cold exposure is a rounding error next to 8,000 steps (~300 cal) — do it for alertness if you enjoy it, not for fat loss.

Stimulant reality: caffeine plus green-tea extract raise expenditure ~50-100 cal/day, fading as tolerance builds in 1-2 weeks. "Metabolism booster" supplements (raspberry ketones, CLA, chromium) show ~0-2 lb effects in trials — statistically real, practically nothing, at $30-60/month. The honest BMR boosters remain unglamorous: more muscle (+6 cal/lb/day), more protein, more sleep, no crash diets.
