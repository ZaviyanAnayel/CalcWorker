US Overtime Rules Under the FLSA

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), non-exempt covered employees in the United States must receive overtime pay for all hours worked over 40 in a workweek at a rate not less than time-and-a-half (1.5 times) their regular hourly rate. Certain states like California also mandate daily overtime for hours worked exceeding 8 in a single calendar day.

⏰ Federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) Overtime Regulations

Under United States federal law (29 U.S.C. § 207), covered non-exempt employees must receive overtime pay for hours worked in excess of 40 hours per standard 7-day workweek at a rate not less than time-and-a-half (1.5×) their regular rate of pay:

  • Regular Rate of Pay: The hourly rate computed by dividing total non-overtime earnings (including nondiscretionary bonuses, shift differentials, and commissions) by total hours worked in that workweek.
  • Daily vs. Weekly Overtime: Federal law only mandates overtime after 40 weekly hours. Certain states (such as California and Nevada) require daily overtime for any hours worked beyond 8 hours in a single workday, and double-time (2.0×) for hours beyond 12.
  • Exempt vs. Non-Exempt: Executive, administrative, and professional employees earning above federal salary thresholds are exempt from overtime protections.

📐 How It Works: Mathematical Formulas & Methodology

FLSA Time-and-a-Half & Double Time Computation Verified Calculation Model
Core Formula:
Total Gross Pay = (40 × R_regular) + (H_OT × 1.5 × R_regular) + (H_DT × 2.0 × R_regular)
In Plain English: Calculates overtime earnings under FLSA guidelines: standard pay for the first 40 hours, plus 1.5× base hourly rate for all qualifying hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek.
Mathematical Variables & Inputs:
  • R_regular: Regular Hourly Rate of Pay (Base wage plus non-discretionary bonuses divided by total hours)
  • H_OT: Overtime Hours worked beyond 40 hours in a 7-consecutive-day workweek (1.5x Multiplier)
  • H_DT: Double Time Hours worked (Applicable under California law for hours exceeding 12 in a single day or 8 hours on the 7th consecutive day)

Follow this real-world example to calculate or verify your numbers manually:

  1. Base Hourly Wage: $28.00 / hour. Workweek: 52 Total Hours worked.
  2. Regular Wages (First 40 hours): 40 × $28.00 = $1,120.00.

🏛️ US Regulatory & Industry Benchmark: The Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) mandates overtime compensation for all non-exempt employees working more than 40 hours in a defined 168-hour workweek. Under California Labor Code Section 510, daily overtime is legally required: 1.5x rate for hours worked beyond 8 up to 12 in a single workday, and 2.0x double time for all hours worked beyond 12 in a workday.

A widespread misconception among hourly workers is that working overtime 'bumps you into a higher tax bracket where you make less money.' Under progressive US federal tax brackets, only the dollars earned within the higher tax tier are taxed at the higher marginal rate; your previous earnings remain taxed at their lower baseline rates. Overtime work always increases your net cash take-home pay.

Employers must calculate the 'regular rate of pay' accurately by including non-discretionary performance bonuses, attendance incentives, and shift differentials into the overtime base rate. If an employee earns $25/hr plus a $200 weekly production bonus over 50 hours, the bonus must be blended into the hourly rate ($29/hr) before computing the 1.5x overtime multiplier.

Strategic Practical Questions

Can an employer offer 'comp time' instead of paying overtime wages?

In the private sector, private employers cannot substitute compensatory time off (comp time) in lieu of paying overtime wages to non-exempt employees covered by the FLSA, even if the worker requests it. Public sector municipal and government agencies are permitted to offer comp time under strict statutory agreements.

Does working on a weekend or holiday automatically entitle me to overtime pay?

Under federal law, no. The FLSA does not require overtime pay simply for working on Saturdays, Sundays, or recognized federal holidays unless those hours push your total weekly working hours beyond 40 hours in that designated payroll cycle.

⏱️ US Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) Overtime Pay Architecture

The Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) mandates that non-exempt employees who work more than 40 hours in a standard seven-day workweek must receive overtime pay at a rate not less than one and one-half times their Regular Rate of Pay ('Time and a Half').

Under federal law, the regular rate of pay must include non-discretionary bonuses, shift differentials, and performance commissions. In California, overtime rules are more stringent: workers receive time and a half after 8 hours in a single workday and Double Time (2.0x) after 12 hours.

⚡ Overtime Rights & Exemption Auditing

  • Audit Your FLSA Exemption Status: Being paid on a salary does NOT automatically make you exempt from overtime; employers must satisfy both the Salary Basis test ($844/week) AND specific executive, administrative, or professional job duties tests.
  • Ensure Non-Discretionary Bonuses Are Included: If you earn a monthly attendance or production bonus, your employer must retroactively re-calculate your regular rate of pay and pay additional overtime wages on the blended rate.
  • Track Unauthorized Overtime: Under 29 CFR § 785.11, if an employer knows or has reason to believe you are working overtime, they must legally pay you, even if the overtime was not pre-authorized.
