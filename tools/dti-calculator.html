📐 How It Works: Mathematical Formulas & Methodology

Dual-Tier Underwriting Debt-to-Income Formulations Verified Calculation Model
Core Formula:
\text{Front-End DTI} = \frac{\text{Proposed PITI}}{\text{Gross Income}} \times 100\% \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Back-End DTI} = \frac{\text{PITI} + \sum \text{Debts}}{\text{Gross Income}} \times 100\%
In Plain English: Calculates the percentage of verified gross monthly earnings committed to housing debt alone versus all contractual monthly liabilities combined.
Mathematical Variables & Inputs:
  • PITI: Proposed monthly principal, interest, property taxes, hazard insurance, and HOA dues.
  • Debts: Minimum contractual monthly obligations (auto loans, student debt, credit cards, child support).
  • Fannie/Freddie Limits: Conventional standard 28% front-end / 36% back-end (up to 45%-50% with automated DU/LP underwriting).
  • FHA Limits: Standard 31% front-end / 43% back-end (up to 56.9% back-end with TOTAL Mortgage Scorecard approval).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Front-End vs Back-End DTI?

Front-end DTI measures housing costs only (PITI) divided by gross income. Back-end DTI includes housing plus all recurring debts.

What is the maximum DTI for conventional loans?

Standard is 36%, but Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac automated systems can approve up to 43-45%.

Do utilities and phone bills count in DTI?

No. Living expenses like utilities and groceries do not appear on credit reports and are excluded.

How can I lower my DTI?

Pay off revolving credit cards, refinance car notes, or increase verified household income.

📊 Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio Underwriting: Front-End vs. Back-End Limits

Your Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio is the single most important metric mortgage underwriters evaluate when determining loan eligibility. DTI measures what percentage of your gross monthly income is consumed by mandatory recurring monthly debt obligations.

Underwriters evaluate two separate DTI benchmarks: the Front-End Ratio (strictly housing expenses including PITI and HOA dues) and the Back-End Ratio (housing expenses plus student loans, minimum credit card payments, auto loans, and child support).

⚡ Techniques to Rapidly Lower Your DTI Ratio

  • Pay Off Small Installment Debts With <10 Months Left: Fannie Mae guidelines allow underwriters to exclude installment loans (like auto loans) from your back-end DTI if 10 or fewer monthly payments remain.
  • Eliminate Low-Balance Credit Cards: A credit card with a $600 balance carrying a $45 minimum monthly payment hurts your mortgage borrowing power by approximately $7,500; paying it off immediately clears that monthly debt capacity.
  • Consolidate Revolving Debt Into Fixed Term Loans: Lowering overall monthly debt payments expands available purchasing power dollar-for-dollar under front-end guidelines.
