📐 How It Works: Mathematical Formulas & Methodology

Personal Balance Sheet Accounting Equation Verified Calculation Model
Core Formula:
\text{Net Worth} = \sum (\text{Liquid Assets} + \text{Investments} + \text{Real Estate Equity} + \text{Personal Property}) - \sum \text{All Liabilities}
In Plain English: Sums total fair market value of all financial and physical assets and subtracts total outstanding mortgage, auto, student, and credit card debt.
Mathematical Variables & Inputs:
  • Assets: Cash equivalents, brokerage portfolios, 401(k)/IRAs, primary/investment real estate market value, vehicles, and business equity.
  • Liabilities: Mortgage balances, student loans, auto financing, personal loans, and credit card revolving balances.
  • Stanley-Danko Benchmark: Expected Wealth = (Age × Pre-Tax Annual Realized Household Income) ÷ 10.

📝 Step-by-Step Practical Calculation Example

Follow this real-world example to calculate or verify your numbers manually:

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the definition of Net Worth?

Net worth is the total financial value of an individual or household, calculated by taking the monetary value of everything you own (assets) and subtracting everything you owe (debts/liabilities).

What is Liquid Net Worth?

Liquid net worth only accounts for assets that can be converted to cash quickly without significant transaction friction or haircuts (cash, savings, stocks, index funds, crypto), excluding illiquid real estate and vehicles.

What is the average and median net worth in the US?

According to Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances data, the median US household net worth is approximately $192,000, while the mean (average) net worth exceeds $1,000,000 due to high-net-worth distribution skew.

How often should I calculate my net worth?

Auditing your personal balance sheet quarterly or semi-annually is optimal for tracking savings rates, mortgage principal paydowns, and investment compounding.

💎 Personal Net Worth Architecture: Asset Liquidity, Debt Valuation & Wealth Velocity

Net worth is the ultimate scorecard of personal financial health, defined as Total Assets (everything you own of economic value) minus Total Liabilities (all outstanding debts owed to creditors). Tracking net worth shifts your financial focus from income alone to lasting balance sheet equity.

A comprehensive personal balance sheet separates assets into three distinct liquidity tiers: Liquid Cash Reserves (checking, savings, money markets), Invested Wealth (401(k), IRAs, taxable brokerage accounts), and Use Assets (primary home equity, vehicles).
