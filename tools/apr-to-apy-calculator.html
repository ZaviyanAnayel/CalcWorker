📐 How It Works: Mathematical Formulas & Methodology

Effective Annual Yield (APY) Compounding Transformation Verified Calculation Model
Core Formula:
\text{APY} = \left(1 + \frac{\text{APR}}{n}\right)^n - 1 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \text{APR} = n \left[ (1 + \text{APY})^{1/n} - 1 \right]
In Plain English: Converts nominal APR into effective APY based on compounding frequency n cycles per calendar year.
Mathematical Variables & Inputs:
  • APR: Nominal Annual Percentage Rate (simple annual stated interest rate).
  • APY: Annual Percentage Yield (effective annual return accounting for intra-year compounding).
  • n: Compounding frequency per year (365 for daily, 12 for monthly, 4 for quarterly).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between APR and APY?

APR (Annual Percentage Rate) is the simple annual interest rate without compounding. APY (Annual Percentage Yield) reflects the true annual return taking compound interest into account.

Why is APY always higher than APR?

Because interest earned in earlier compounding periods begins earning interest itself in subsequent periods (interest-on-interest), pushing the effective yield higher than the nominal rate.

Why do credit card companies quote APR while savings banks quote APY?

Lenders quote APR on loans and credit cards to make the borrowing cost appear lower, while banks quote APY on savings accounts and CDs to make the return appear higher.

How does daily compounding compare to monthly compounding?

Daily compounding yields slightly more interest than monthly compounding. For example, a 5.00% APR yields 5.116% APY with monthly compounding and 5.127% APY with daily compounding.

🔄 APR vs. APY Mathematics: Compounding Frequency & True Yield Equations

Annual Percentage Rate (APR) and Annual Percentage Yield (APY) are two standardized methods for quoting interest, but they represent vastly different financial realities. APR represents the nominal simple interest rate without accounting for intra-year compounding, whereas APY reflects the true effective annual rate earned or paid when compounding interest is factored in.

In banking, institutions quote APY on deposit accounts (savings, CDs) to make yields look higher, and quote APR on loans and credit cards to make borrowing costs appear lower. Under federal Truth in Savings and Truth in Lending rules, understanding compounding conversions protects consumer wealth.

⚡ Compounding Rate Optimization Principles

  • Compare APY to APY for Savings: When shopping for High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSA) or Certificates of Deposit (CDs), always compare APYs to normalize differing compounding schedules.
  • Watch Out for Credit Card Daily Compounding: Credit cards compound interest daily (n = 365); a 24.00% stated APR actually costs 27.11% APY if balances revolve continuously.
  • Continuous Compounding Limit: As compounding frequency approaches infinity, effective yield converges to Euler's limit: APY = e^r - 1.
