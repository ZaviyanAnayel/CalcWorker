₿ IRS Cryptocurrency Tax Rules & Cost Basis Accounting

Under IRS Notice 2014-21, digital assets (Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, and altcoins) are legally classified as property. Every trade, swap, or sale represents a taxable disposition that must be reported on IRS Form 8949:

  • Short-Term vs. Long-Term Capital Gains: Tokens held for 365 days or less are taxed as short-term capital gains at your ordinary federal income tax bracket (10% to 37%). Assets held longer than one full year qualify for long-term rates (0%, 15%, or 20%).
  • Cost Basis Accounting Methods: First-In, First-Out (FIFO) is the default IRS method. Specific Identification (SpecID) and Highest-In, First-Out (HIFO) can minimize taxable net gains if thorough transaction logs are maintained.
  • Deductible Exchange & Gas Fees: Network transaction fees (Ethereum gas) and centralized exchange commissions (Coinbase, Kraken, Binance) reduce your gross proceeds or increase your cost basis, legally lowering net taxable capital gains.

Strategic Practical Questions

Do I have to pay taxes if I just hold crypto in a hardware wallet?

No. Purchasing cryptocurrency with US dollars and holding it in a self-custody cold storage hardware wallet or centralized exchange is not a taxable event. Taxes are only triggered upon a disposal: selling for fiat, trading for another token, or spending crypto on goods and services.

Can I harvest crypto capital losses to offset stock market gains?

Yes. Crypto capital losses offset capital gains dollar-for-dollar across all asset classes, including traditional stocks, index funds, and real estate. Additionally, up to $3,000 of net capital losses can be deducted directly against ordinary employment income each tax year.

🪙 Cryptocurrency Capital Gains: IRS Notice 2014-21 Tax Rules & Cost Basis

Under IRS Notice 2014-21, the Internal Revenue Service treats virtual currencies, cryptocurrencies, and digital tokens strictly as *property*, not as foreign fiat currency. Every cryptocurrency transaction—including selling crypto for US Dollars, trading one crypto for another (e.g. BTC to ETH), or spending crypto to purchase goods—is a taxable disposal event.

Taxpayers must track Cost Basis (original purchase price plus exchange transaction fees) and calculate capital gains or losses using specific identification accounting methods: First-In, First-Out (FIFO), Highest-In, First-Out (HIFO), or Specific Identification.

⚡ Cryptocurrency Tax Optimization Strategies

  • Crypto Wash Sale Exemption Status: Cryptocurrencies are currently classified as property rather than securities under IRC § 1091, allowing investors to sell at a loss and immediately repurchase without violating the 30-day wash sale rule (pending statutory legislation).
  • Track Gas Fees as Basis Additions: Blockchain transaction gas fees paid to execute buys add directly to your asset cost basis, while transfer fees on sales reduce your net taxable proceeds.
  • Report Staking and Airdrops as Ordinary Income: Staking rewards and airdrop tokens are taxed as ordinary gross income at their fair market value at the exact time custody is received, not capital gains.
