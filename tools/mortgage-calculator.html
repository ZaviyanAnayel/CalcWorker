How to Calculate Your Monthly Mortgage Payment (PITI)

Purchasing a home is the largest capital acquisition most Americans make. Your monthly payment is not just loan principal and interest; it includes mandatory property taxes, insurance, and private mortgage insurance (PMI). These components combine into the industry-standard acronym PITI.

Understanding the Components of PITI

  • Principal: The money that directly repays your borrowed loan balance.
  • Interest: The cost charged by the lender to finance your mortgage.
  • Taxes: County or municipal real estate taxes held in an escrow account.
  • Insurance: Homeowners insurance policy covering structural hazards.
  • PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance): Mandatory on conventional loans with down payments below 20% (LTV above 80%). Automatically cancels under federal law once your loan reaches 78% LTV.

The Standard Fixed-Rate Amortization Formula

M = P × [ r(1 + r)^n ] / [ (1 + r)^n - 1 ]

M = Monthly Principal & Interest | P = Principal Loan Amount
r = Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate / 12) | n = Total Months (Years × 12)

The 28/36 Qualifying Rule

Lenders use the 28/36 debt-to-income rule to evaluate loan affordability. Your housing payment (PITI) should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income, while your total debt obligations (housing + auto loans + student debt + minimum credit card dues) should remain below 36%.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is a good mortgage rate in 2026?

In 2026, the average 30-year fixed rate is ~6.8%. Borrowers with credit scores above 740 and 20%+ down payment can often qualify for rates 0.3–0.5% below average.

How much house can I afford?

Use the 28/36 rule: your monthly mortgage payment (PITI) should be no more than 28% of gross monthly income. Total debt (including car, student loans) should not exceed 36%.

What is PMI and when does it go away?

PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance) is required when your down payment is less than 20%. It typically costs 0.5%–1% annually. PMI is automatically cancelled when your LTV ratio reaches 78% — usually in the first 7–10 years of a 30-year mortgage.

30-year vs 15-year mortgage — which is better?

A 15-year mortgage saves enormous interest — often $100,000+. But monthly payments are ~40% higher. A 30-year provides cash flow flexibility. Many financial advisors recommend a 30-year mortgage and making extra principal payments when affordable.

📐 How It Works: Mathematical Formulas & Methodology

Mortgage Payment (PITI) & Amortization Mathematical Framework Verified Calculation Model
Core Formula:
M = P [ (r(1 + r)ⁿ ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ - 1) ] + T [monthly] + I [monthly] + PMI [monthly]
In Plain English: Your total monthly payment combines principal repayment, interest calculated on the outstanding loan balance, one-twelfth of annual property taxes, homeowners insurance, and PMI (if down payment is under 20%).
Mathematical Variables & Inputs:
  • M: Total Monthly Housing Payment (PITI)
  • P: Principal Loan Amount (Purchase Price minus Down Payment)
  • r: Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Percentage Rate APR ÷ 12)
  • n: Total Number of Monthly Amortization Payments (Loan Term in Years × 12)
  • T_mo: Monthly Property Tax (Annual Assessed Millage Rate ÷ 12)
  • I_mo: Monthly Hazard/Homeowners Insurance Premium (Annual Cost ÷ 12)
  • PMI_mo: Private Mortgage Insurance (Applied when Down Payment is under 20% of Home Value)

📝 Step-by-Step Practical Calculation Example

Follow this real-world example to calculate or verify your numbers manually:

  1. Purchase Price: $400,000 with 10% Down Payment ($40,000). Principal Loan Balance P = $360,000.
  2. Interest Rate: 6.85% Fixed for 30 Years (n = 360). Monthly rate r = 0.0685 / 12 = 0.0057083.
  3. Base Principal & Interest: M_base = 360,000 × [0.0057083 × (1.0057083)^360] / [(1.0057083)^360 - 1] = $2,359.18 / month.
  4. Escrow Additions: Property Tax = $416.67/mo (1.25% annual), Insurance = $125.00/mo, PMI = $195.00/mo (0.65% rate).
  5. Total Monthly Obligation (PITI): $2,359.18 + $416.67 + $125.00 + $195.00 = $3,095.85.
💡 Pro Tip for Solving Complex Cases: To eliminate PMI early, track your local home equity. When your loan balance hits 80% of your home's appraised value, submit a formal written cancellation request to your lender under the Homeowners Protection Act.
🏛️ US Regulatory & Industry Benchmark: In 2026, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) and Fannie Mae guidelines establish a maximum conforming front-end debt-to-income (DTI) ratio of 28% for housing costs, with back-end DTI capped at 36% to 43%. Conforming loan limits benchmark at $766,550 in baseline US counties. PMI automatically cancels by federal mandate under the Homeowners Protection Act once loan-to-value (LTV) reaches 78%.

Understanding the mathematical velocity of amortization is the key to minimizing total interest expense over a 30-year lifecycle. During the initial 5 to 7 years of a standard mortgage, over 70% of each monthly installment is directed toward interest rather than principal retirement. For instance, on a $360,000 note at 6.85%, your very first monthly payment transfers $2,055 to the lender in interest alone, with only $304.18 reducing the loan balance.

Homeowners can achieve substantial interest reduction by applying targeted extra principal prepayments. Adding just one extra monthly payment of $2,359 per calendar year eliminates approximately 6.5 years from a 30-year amortization schedule, saving over $98,000 in cumulative interest charges. Furthermore, monitoring your property's equity allows you to petition your loan servicer to cancel Private Mortgage Insurance at 80% LTV rather than waiting for the automatic 78% milestone, freeing up $150 to $250 per month in disposable cash flow.

Strategic Practical Questions

How does an adjustable-rate mortgage (5/1 or 7/1 ARM) compare to a 30-year fixed loan in 2026?

A 5/1 ARM offers a reduced introductory teaser rate for the initial 60 months, after which the interest rate adjusts annually based on the Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) plus a predetermined margin (usually 2.75%). While ARMs lower initial payments by $200–$400 monthly, they introduce interest rate risk if benchmark rates elevate at the adjustment window. Fixed-rate mortgages provide absolute budget predictability for buyers intending to reside in the home beyond 5 years.

What is the difference between mortgage APR and the note interest rate?

The note interest rate is the base cost of borrowing the principal balance, used to compute your monthly P&I payment. The Annual Percentage Rate (APR) reflects the true cost of credit by incorporating mandatory financing expenses into the interest calculation, including lender origination points, underwriting fees, prepaid interest, and mortgage broker compensation. Comparing loan offers using APR rather than note rate ensures transparent evaluation of closing fees.

🏠 Residential Mortgage Structuring, PITI Breakdown & Principal Amortization

A conventional residential mortgage is a multi-decade amortized contract where monthly payments are apportioned across four distinct components: Principal, Interest, Property Taxes, and Homeowners Hazard Insurance (PITI). In the initial decade of a 30-year note, more than 65% of every monthly installment services compounding interest.

Borrowers putting down less than 20% equity incur Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI), which adds $100 to $300 monthly without building home equity. Exploring 15-year terms or accelerating principal debt reduction saves hundreds of thousands of dollars in lifetime interest charges.

⚡ Mortgage Wealth Maximization Strategies

  • Make Bi-Weekly Half-Payments: Dividing your monthly payment by two and submitting it every two weeks results in 26 half-payments (13 full payments per year), cutting 4 to 6 years off a 30-year mortgage.
  • Target 80% LTV to Eliminate PMI: Under the federal Homeowners Protection Act of 1998, you can formally request PMI cancellation once your loan balance reaches 80% of original appraised value.
  • Verify Escrow Cushion Adjustments: Review your annual mortgage servicer escrow statement (RESPA Section 10) to catch billing errors on county property tax and hazard insurance cushions.
